# Your Software Won't Matter in 5 Years (Here's What to Do Instead)

**Source:** [Frank Nillard - YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O8IvlUNIQGY)  
**Date:** October 25, 2026  
**Duration:** 12:57  
**Transcribed:** 2,429 words

---

## Executive Summary

**The Crisis:** AI can rebuild entire software suites in days. Someone just reverse-engineered Adobe using Claude and gave it away free.

**The Problem:** If code itself isn't defensible anymore, what is?

**The Answer:** Six things—and none of them are code.

---

## The Moment We're In

### What's Happening Right Now

We're witnessing a wave of AI-powered software reproduction:
- Someone rebuilt the entire Adobe app suite with Claude and open-sourced it
- Samsung TV source code revealed hidden surveillance (counting baby cries, dog barks)
- Startups keep following the same path: wrappers → tools → automations → AI agents

### Why This Cycle Keeps Repeating

**Pattern:**
1. AI models have limitations
2. Startups build products to fill the gaps (e.g., "AI that reads PDFs")
3. When models improve and add that feature natively, startups die
4. Everyone chases the next gap

**Example:** PDF reading products existed until ChatGPT added PDF reading. Now they're gone.

---

## What's Actually Dying

### Single-Feature Tools

- Tools that do only one thing
- Now just a skill inside Claude (or another model)
- Hardcoded business rules that break when better models arrive

### The Adobe Wake-Up Call

The person who rebuilt Adobe said:
> "It's not anywhere close to ready. My roadmap puts it at 45% ready for real work. Photoshop files it saves can't be reliably opened in Photoshop yet."

**The lesson:** There's a massive gap between "looks pretty as a Claude artifact" and "actual production software."

**Real software:**
- Handles real data reliably
- Works every single day
- Improves continuously with feedback
- Gets fixed when bugs are discovered
- Responds to customer needs

**Claude artifact:**
- Looks nice on the surface
- One-shot creation
- No integration with real systems
- Can't be maintained or improved

**Real truth:** You can't one-shot an entire software product with a prompt. Not yet.

## If Code Isn't a Moat Anymore, What Is?

### The Six Defensible Moats (In Order of Importance)

#### #1: **Obsession**

**Why it's foundational:** Everything else takes years to build.

**Reality check:** You're building a company, not just code.
- Bringing in teammates
- Accepting payments
- Managing users
- Scaling operations

**The truth:** You'll quit before minimum success if you don't genuinely care about the problem.

**Implication:** Pick something you'll still find interesting in year 3.

---

#### #2: **Sell the Result, Not the Tool** 🔥

**This is the biggest paradigm shift.**

**Old model:** Sell software to companies
- "Here's a tool to use"
- Charge per login or SaaS subscription
- Compete with competitors who have the same tool

**New model: "Full-Stack AI Company" or "Service-as-Software"**

You become the service provider, but powered by AI:

```
Front-facing: You look like an agency
  - Sell the outcome (more leads, faster invoices, delivered ads)
  - Charge like a service (because you're selling results)
  - Client pays for problem-solving, not a login

Back-end: Running on your own software + AI
  - Every job you do manually, you automate bit by bit
  - Over time, builds into a well-oiled machine
  - Most work runs on its own
```

**Why this works:**
- You charge agency prices (high)
- You scale like software (unlimited clients)
- You get best of both worlds
- Exit valuations: self-serving agencies command premium multiples

**Real examples:**
- Instead of selling a tool to law firms → become the law firm (AI-powered)
- Own all the revenue, not just the software budget
- Deliver the outcome they actually want

**Industry validation:**
- Y Combinator calls these "Full-Stack AI Companies"
- Foundation Capital calls it "Service-as-Software"
- Opportunity market: $4.6 trillion (services market), not just software

---

#### #3: **Data from Actually Doing the Work**

**The problem with general models:** Claude, GPT, Gemini all work on general understanding.

**What you actually need:** Industry-specific edge cases, workflows, patterns.

**The moat:**
- You know what workflows fail, what succeeds
- You understand customer-specific patterns
- You know which edge cases matter

**Why manual work creates unfair advantage:**
- Industry veteran (10 years) might know their own company best
- But only sees their own company's data
- If you run across 10 companies, you see patterns no insider can
- This cross-company insight is a real moat

**Implication:** You have to do the work yourself (at least initially) to gather this data.

---

#### #4: **Connections in the Industry**

**Not necessary, but very important for speed-to-market.**

**What it provides:**
- Being in the room on sales calls
- Trusted relationships at conferences
- Network of people who know you

**Why it's defensible:**
- Competitor can copy your software in a weekend
- Competitor cannot copy 2 years of relationships
- Trust takes time to build

---

#### #5: **Community**

**Definition:** People who learn with you, give feedback, stick around.

**Why it matters:**
- They make your product better (free feedback engine)
- Hard to steal because they're not there just for the software
- They're there for each other, for the group
- Network effects: loyalty to the community, not the tool

---

#### #6: **Distribution** 🚀

**This might be the most important one (alongside obsession).**

**Why:** 
- Anyone can build now
- Building is no longer a moat (just requires AI prompt)
- One viral video can give your idea to competitors overnight
- If competitor posts viral video and you don't → all users go to them

**Distribution gets you the shots, but the other 5 make it last.**

## The Key Insight: Look 5-10 Years Ahead

### The Question That Matters Most

**"How will people and businesses be using digital tools in 5-10 years?"**

This is what you should be building toward, not today's habits.

### The Shift Already Happening

**Email:**
- You write with Claude
- Recipients reply with Claude
- Essentially: AI talking to AI, with humans approving

**Shopping:**
- Instead of clicking through 5 pages
- AI agent in WhatsApp asks what you like
- Picks option and buys it
- You never open a website

### The Edge as a Predictor

**Insight:** Watch the 0.01% of the population at the bleeding edge.
- They predict the future for everyone else
- You see what's coming before it hits mainstream
- Tech edges show where the market is heading

### Where Builders Are Missing It

**Current mindset:** Everyone's building apps with pretty interfaces.

**Reality:** Most actual work happens inside Claude (or AI agents), not inside apps.

**Implication:** The winners won't be companies with the prettiest interface.

**Winners will be:** Companies whose services can be reached and used through APIs or MCP (Model Context Protocol).

**The businesses that win:** Agent-accessible, not human-interface-dependent.

## If You're Starting From Zero Today: The Playbook

### Step 1: Pick Your Industry

Choose something you:
- Are genuinely obsessed with
- Already understand (or willing to learn fast over years)

### Step 2: Sit with the People in It

- Ideally, be one of them
- Reach out to others in the network
- Find the problem causing them the most pain

### Step 3: Solve It Scrappily (By Hand First)

**Don't build a proper solution yet.**
- Avoid just building an automated pipeline
- Solve it manually first, messily
- Use low-code/flow code if needed
- Get it working fast, not perfect

### Step 4: Get Paid as a Service

**Don't sell the software yet.**
- Sell the outcome
- Charge like a service (agency pricing)
- Money is the only real proof
- You can iterate fast this way

### Step 5: While Doing the Work, Collect Data & Build Relationships

**In parallel:**
- Collect industry-specific data (edge cases, patterns, workflows)
- Build trust with customers and network
- Develop the obsession deeper
- Create the community
- Only then build actual software

### What You Have When Software Time Comes

By the time you build the software product, you already have:
- ✅ Some paying customers
- ✅ Industry connections (trust)
- ✅ Real data (edge cases, patterns)
- ✅ Deep obsession
- ✅ Proven problem-solution fit

**Then, when the next AI model makes software 10x easier to copy:** Doesn't matter, because software was never your moat.

## The Core Reframe

**Old thinking:** Code is the defensible asset. Build fast, move fast, own the tech.

**New thinking:** Code is commoditized. The defensible assets are:
- Understanding the problem deeper than anyone
- People who trust you to solve it
- Speed to market through distribution
- Data from doing the work
- Relationships
- Community
- Obsession

**Bottom line:**

> "Stop building basic AI wrappers or basic workflow wrappers. The advantage isn't anymore who can code the fastest. It's who understands the problem the deepest and who people trust to solve it and who gets to own the distribution quickest."

**Knowledge → Zero, Trust → 100**

This video is the other side of the coin:

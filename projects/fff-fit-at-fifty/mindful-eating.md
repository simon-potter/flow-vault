# Mindful Eating & Fat Loss

Science-based approaches to sustainable weight loss and breaking bad eating habits.

## Mindful Eating Foundations

- **Slow down.** Put fork down between bites, chew thoroughly (20-30 times per bite). Gives brain time to register fullness signals (~20 minutes).
- **Eliminate distractions.** Eat away from screens, work, or reading. Single-task meals so you actually taste and notice what you're eating.
- **Use smaller plates.** Psychological trigger — smaller plates feel fuller with same portion, reducing overfilling tendency.

## Breaking Snacking Habits

### Identify Triggers, Not Willpower
Track when snacking occurs — is it:
- Boredom
- Stress
- Habit (time-based)
- Actual hunger

Address the trigger, not the symptom.

### Out of Sight, Out of Mind
Remove tempting snacks from easy reach. Keep in cupboard, not on counter or desk.

### Replace, Don't Restrict
Swap high-calorie snacks with lower-calorie alternatives (nuts, fruit, veggies). Make better option more convenient.

### Drink Water First
Thirst often masquerades as hunger. Have glass of water, wait 10 minutes before deciding if actually hungry.

## Practical Strategies

### Scheduled Eating
Eat on consistent schedule. Skipping meals → intense hunger → overeating/snacking.

### The 15-Minute Rule
When cravings hit, wait 15 minutes. Often they pass once distracted with something else.

### Prep Snacks Intentionally
If snacking, portion first (don't eat from bag). Conscious choice feels different from mindless grazing.

### Hunger Hierarchy
Before eating, ask: Am I physically hungry, or eating for comfort/boredom/stress?
- Real hunger grows over time
- Emotional eating is sudden and specific to one food

## Environmental Design

### Make Healthy Snacks Visible
Keep cut veggies, fruit, or nuts at eye level in fridge. Make default option the good option.

### Add Friction to Bad Snacks
If keeping them, make inconvenient — locked in drawer, back of cupboard, or not in house at all.

### Buddy System or Accountability
Track snacking for a week without judgment — just data. Visibility alone often breaks the habit.

## What Usually Works

Most people find success combining:

1. **Trigger awareness** — Why are you eating?
2. **Environmental design** — Make it harder to snack
3. **Replacement routines** — Do something else when urge hits (walk, text friend, stretch)

**Willpower alone rarely wins.** Start with one or two strategies, not all at once. Small changes compound.

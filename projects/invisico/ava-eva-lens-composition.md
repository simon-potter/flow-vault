# AVA/EVA — Composed as a Lens Intervention

## Core Question

**What are the plausible futures for this decision, and what are the full costs/risks if things go wrong?**

This is a **scenario-mapping + consequence-testing intervention** that examines high-stakes, irreversible decisions through multiple time horizons and consequence lenses.

---

## Lens Composition (8 lenses, 4 families)

| Step | Lens | Family | Question |
|------|------|--------|----------|
| Context | Evidence | Reality | What is the actual evidence for the key assumptions? |
| Map 1 | Opportunity Cost | Strategic | What futures are foreclosed by choosing this? |
| Map 2 | Compounding | Strategic | Does this compound over time, positively or negatively? |
| Test 1 | One Year | Temporal | Where does this leave you in twelve months? |
| Test 2 | Five Years | Temporal | What trajectory does this set? |
| Test 3 | Deathbed | Temporal | Would you regret saying no more than saying yes? |
| Test 4 | Identity | Psychological | Does this align with who you are becoming? |
| Test 5 | Counterfactual (Relief Test) | Reality | If this option disappeared, what is the first feeling? |

**Family balance:**
- Reality: 2 lenses (Evidence, Counterfactual) — testing assumptions and emotional reality
- Strategic: 2 lenses (Opportunity Cost, Compounding) — long-term economic view
- Temporal: 3 lenses (1yr, 5yr, Deathbed) — multiple horizons reveal different aspects
- Psychological: 1 lens (Identity) — alignment with core self

**Why this composition:** By hitting 3 temporal lenses, AVA/EVA forces the decision through multiple time lenses, revealing whether the same choice looks good at every horizon or whether regret sets in later. Reality lenses test whether assumptions hold. Strategic lenses reveal what's being given up and whether it compounds well.

## Lens-by-Lens Breakdown

### 1. Evidence (Reality)
**Question:** What is the actual evidence for the key assumption in this decision?

**User input:** State the key assumption (e.g., "This role will increase autonomy" or "The market will grow 20% annually").

**Observation produced:** Confidence level (e.g., "High confidence — data supports; Low confidence — anecdotal only; Uncertain — conflicting data").

**Signal:** Reveals whether the decision is built on sand or rock.

### 2. Opportunity Cost (Strategic)
**Question:** What futures or options are foreclosed by choosing this?

**User input:** Name 2-3 alternatives that become unavailable if you choose this path.

**Observation produced:** Named alternatives + cost direction (e.g., "Closes option: Stay at current company; Cost: High — other offer expires in 30 days").

**Signal:** Reveals what you're giving up; often the biggest hidden cost.

### 3. Compounding (Strategic)
**Question:** Does this decision compound over time, positively or negatively?

**User input:** Project forward — does this lock you into a path that gets better or worse over time?

**Observation produced:** Directional with horizon (e.g., "Compounds positively by year 3 as skills deepen; Compounds negatively if you're trapped in low-growth role").

**Signal:** Reveals whether you're making a compounding win or a compounding trap.

### 4. One Year (Temporal)
**Question:** Where does this leave you in twelve months?

**User input:** Describe a realistic day-in-the-life at the 1-year mark if you choose this.

**Observation produced:** Named state (e.g., "Exhausted but competent; Regretful and looking for exit; Energized and growing").

**Signal:** Short-term consequences become concrete; reveals whether honeymoon fades.

### 5. Five Years (Temporal)
**Question:** What trajectory does this set?

**User input:** Zoom out — what is this decision a step toward?

**Observation produced:** Named trajectory (e.g., "Leadership path; Specialization trap; Creative fulfillment; Golden handcuffs").

**Signal:** Reveals the long-term shape of your life if you follow this path.

### 6. Deathbed (Temporal)
**Question:** Would you regret saying no to this more than saying yes?

**User input:** Imagine yourself at the end of life. Which path would you regret not taking?

**Observation produced:** Directional (e.g., "Regret no more than yes; Regret no significantly more; Regret yes more; Uncertain").

**Signal:** The deepest test — cuts through rationalization to core values.

### 7. Identity (Psychological)
**Question:** Does this align with who you are becoming?

**User input:** Describe who you're trying to become; then assess fit.

**Observation produced:** Alignment score (Low/Med/High) + reason.

**Signal:** Reveals whether this is a step toward your best self or away from it.

### 8. Counterfactual / Relief Test (Reality)
**Question:** If this option disappeared tomorrow, what is the first feeling?

**User input:** Imagine it's off the table. What emotion bubbles up?

**Observation produced:** Named feeling (Relief, Dread, Regret, Disappointment, Sadness, Freedom, etc.).

**Signal:** The emotional truth beneath the rational analysis. Relief often signals the decision isn't actually wanted.

## Synthesis Contract

The 8 observations combine into a **decision landscape** that reveals:

1. **Assumption solidity** (Evidence) — Is this built on solid ground or speculation?
2. **What you're trading** (Opportunity Cost) — What doors close?
3. **Long-term shape** (Compounding + One Year + Five Years) — Does this compound well? Does regret set in?
4. **Deepest truth** (Deathbed + Identity) — Does your best self endorse this?
5. **Emotional reality** (Counterfactual) — What does your gut actually feel?

**Synthesis pattern:** If Evidence is weak + Counterfactual is Relief, the decision likely isn't wanted. If Deathbed + Identity align but Compounding is negative, there's a time-horizon mismatch.

**The forcing question:**
> "Knowing the full landscape — [dominant observation from Evidence/Counterfactual/Deathbed] — would you still choose it?"

If the answer changes based on which observation you highlight, that's signal that the observations aren't aligned. That tension is the value.

---

## Output Format

**User receives:**

1. **Landscape summary** (8 observations, one per lens)
2. **Alignment check** (do the lenses agree or conflict?)
3. **Dominant signal** (which observation is strongest/most decisive?)
4. **Forcing question** (knowing this, would you still choose it?)
5. **Actions or reflections** (based on what emerged)

**For analytics:**

- Which lens observation was most influential?
- Did user's confidence increase or decrease?
- Did temporal lenses (1yr/5yr/Deathbed) align or conflict?
- Did emotional signal (Counterfactual) match rational analysis (Evidence/Opportunity Cost)?

## Why This Lens Composition Works for AVA/EVA

**Old framing:** Map scenarios → assign probabilities → calculate EV → script failure triggers.

**New framing:** Test assumptions (Evidence) → reveal what you're losing (Opportunity Cost) → project forward across time (1yr/5yr) → test deepest alignment (Deathbed/Identity) → reality-check emotion (Counterfactual).

**Advantage of lens composition:**
- Lenses are reusable (Evidence appears in other interventions; so does Counterfactual)
- Focus is on observation-gathering, not on EV math (which can obscure the real issues)
- The 8 observations together reveal whether this decision is actually wanted, not just whether it calculates well
- Time-horizon compression (1yr vs 5yr vs Deathbed) reveals whether short-term vs long-term desires conflict

---

## Relationship to Original AVA/EVA

The original AVA/EVA framework (Map Scenarios → Surface Hidden Cards → Assign Probabilities → Calculate EV → Script Failure) is still valid. But as a lens composition, it becomes:

- **Map Scenarios** ← covered by temporal lenses (1yr, 5yr, Deathbed)
- **Surface Hidden Cards** ← covered by Evidence + Falsification lenses
- **Assign Probabilities** ← covered by Compounding + Opportunity Cost lenses
- **Calculate EV** ← synthesized from strategic + temporal observations
- **Script Failure** ← implicit in Deathbed + Identity + Counterfactual (if those are negative, failure is likely)

The lens composition approach trades explicit probability math for a broader observation-gathering process. Which is better depends on the user's needs:
- **Original AVA/EVA:** Better if the user is comfortable with probabilistic thinking and wants to calculate explicit expected values
- **Lens composition:** Better if the user wants to understand the full decision landscape without math

Both are valid; they serve different decision-makers.

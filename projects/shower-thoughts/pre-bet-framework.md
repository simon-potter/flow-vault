# Pre-Bet Framework: Evaluating Which Horse to Ride

**Before you invest time, energy, or credibility into a venture, answer these questions honestly. This is not validation — it's brutal honesty about whether this horse can actually run.**

---

## 1. The Market Reality (Not Your Hope)

**Question:** Who urgently needs this problem solved *today*?

- [ ] Can you name 3–5 specific people/companies who have this pain?
- [ ] Have you talked to them unprompted about their frustration? (Not "would you use this?" but "what are you currently doing about this problem?")
- [ ] Are they actively spending money on workarounds or solutions right now?

**Red flags:**
- "Everyone needs this"
- "They don't know they need it yet"
- You can't name specific people, only market segments
- They're not currently spending money on solutions

**What it means if you can't answer:** You're guessing. You're in the builder's trap. Stop here.

---

## 2. Why You Over Someone Else (Competitive Advantage)

**Question:** Why would someone choose *you* instead of:
- Existing solutions (even if imperfect)
- Competitors
- Doing nothing / solving it manually
- Another founder who might build the same thing

- [ ] Do you have unfair access to customers?
- [ ] Do you have deep domain expertise the market would pay for?
- [ ] Is there a distribution channel only you can reach?
- [ ] Do you have an existing user base / credibility?

**Red flags:**
- "Because our product is better"
- "Because we care more"
- "Because it's cheaper"
- You can't articulate a specific, defensible reason

**What it means if you can't answer:** You're competing on commodity traits. High chance of failure.

---

## 3. The Revenue Mechanism (How Money Actually Enters)

**Question:** How does someone go from "I have this pain" to "I pay you money"?

- [ ] Who is the buyer? (Not the user — the person with the budget)
- [ ] What's the sales cycle? (days? months? years?)
- [ ] What would they have to give up to use you instead of their current solution?
- [ ] How do you reach them? (not "marketing" — specific channels)

**Red flags:**
- "We'll figure out monetization later"
- "We'll sell to enterprises" (but you've never sold enterprise before)
- "It's a marketplace" (without proof of supply and demand)
- You have no specific buyer in mind

**What it means if you can't answer:** You have no business yet. You have a product.

---

## 4. The Leadership Question (The Real Blocker)

**Question:** Does the other leadership have a coherent vision for how this business wins?

- [ ] Can they articulate the market they're going after in one sentence?
- [ ] Do they know who the buyer is?
- [ ] Do they have conviction about the revenue model?
- [ ] Are they willing to pivot if the market tells them they're wrong?

**Red flags (from your situation):**
- "Very disorganised"
- No clear vision for the company
- You're doing lots of work effectively for free
- Decisions are made by hope, not by customer feedback

**What it means if leadership can't answer:** You're rowing a boat with no one at the helm. Even if the market opportunity is real, this team will burn through it.

---

## 5. Your Actual Leverage (What You Control)

**Question:** If this fails, what have you learned or built that transfers to the next thing?

- [ ] Are you learning a market, not just a product?
- [ ] Are you building relationships with customers you can sell to again?
- [ ] Are you developing a skill that's valuable outside this venture?
- [ ] Or are you just doing implementation work that dies if this venture dies?

**Red flags:**
- You're building custom features for one client
- The tech is specific to this product
- You have no customer relationships
- You're not learning anything about the market

**What it means:** This is a consulting gig disguised as a startup. That's fine, but know what it is.

---

## 6. The Time Cost vs. Upside (Brutal Math)

**Question:** Given your financial constraints, what's the minimum viable timeline for this to pay?

- [ ] How much runway does this business have? (months)
- [ ] How long until you know if it's working? (months)
- [ ] What's the upside if it works? (revenue, equity value)
- [ ] What's the opportunity cost? (what else could you do with this time)

**Formula for TrueText specifically:**
```
If TrueText has potentially big upside but the team is disorganised:
- Timeline to product-market fit: 6-12 months (if leadership gets it together)
- Risk of failure due to leadership: 70%+
- Your workload: Likely to increase (you'll be fixing org problems)
- Upside if it works: Real (high-upside venture)
- Opportunity cost: Time away from ventures with clearer vision

Decision framework:
If you're betting on THIS team to get organized → 20% confidence
If you're betting on the MARKET (and planning to replace leadership) → 60% confidence
If you're just the builder while leadership figures it out → Consulting, not founder role
```

---

## 7. The "Walk Away" Test

**Question:** Under what conditions would you quit this?

- [ ] Is there a date when you re-evaluate? (3 months? 6 months?)
- [ ] What metric would tell you to stop? (zero traction? leadership won't listen? you're broke?)
- [ ] Could you actually walk away, or are you emotionally invested?

**Red flags:**
- "I can't walk away, I've invested too much"
- "If I quit, I've made my bed" (this is real, but it's also how you get trapped)
- No re-evaluation date

**What it means:** This is a commitment. Make sure it's the right one.

| **Weighted Total** | 100% | | | |

**Scoring:** 1–5 per factor (1 = "I'm guessing," 5 = "I'm certain")

**Green light:** 4+ average (you have a real business hypothesis)  
**Caution:** 2–4 (you have product, need market validation)  
**Red flag:** <2 (you're building, not betting)

---

## The Decision

**Bet on this horse if:**
- Market reality is clear (score 4+)
- Leadership has conviction (score 4+)
- You can articulate why you win (score 4+)
- Upside justifies the time risk

**Don't bet, or demote to consulting if:**
- Market reality is unclear
- Leadership is disorganised and won't admit it
- You're the only one who knows what you're building
- Upside doesn't justify the opportunity cost

**Red line:** If you're working effectively for free and the leadership can't articulate why, that's not a bet. That's a hole you're digging.

## The Yield Spread Explained: Why is US 0.655% higher?

**1. Duration Premium**
- US 30-year > France 10-year by 20 years
- Longer duration = more rate risk = higher yield required
- **Expected: +0.5-0.7% for 20 extra years of duration**
- ✓ Explains part of the spread

**2. Geopolitical Premium**
- US: $40T debt, Fed locked in high rates, Iran war spiking energy, political uncertainty
- France: EU member, ECB backstop, stable political system
- **Expected: +0.2-0.3% for US risk premium**
- ✓ Explains additional spread

**3. Expected Return on EUR/USD**
- If dollar expected to weaken vs euro: US yields higher to compensate
- If euro expected to weaken vs dollar: France yields should be higher
- Currently: Market pricing modest dollar strength
- **Expected: +0.1% for currency expectations**
- ✓ Explains marginal spread

**Total Expected Spread: +0.8-1.1%**
**Actual Spread: +0.655%**

**Interpretation:** US Treasuries are actually SLIGHTLY CHEAP relative to French OATs
- The spread is compressed
- You're getting paid less for duration and US risk than historical averages
- This suggests: Market is not pricing in full US debt crisis risk yet

## The "When Does Something Break?" Question

### French Perspective:

**OAT Yield Rising 40% YTD signals:**

1. **ECB Tightening Risk**
   - European inflation stickier than expected
   - ECB rate hikes baked into forward curve
   - If ECB cuts before Fed, EUR weakness → OAT yields rise more
   - If ECB stays tight, government refinancing costs explode

2. **French Fiscal Stress**
   - Government debt rising
   - Higher yields = higher debt service costs
   - Political pressure to cut spending or raise taxes
   - Fragile coalition government means delayed decisions

3. **EU Fragmentation Risk**
   - Southern countries (Italy, Spain, Greece) yields rising even faster
   - Spread between Germany and France widening
   - Could trigger ECB intervention (OMT = bond-buying to contain spreads)
   - ECB constraints are real (inflation fighting, German opposition)

**What Breaks:** ECB has to choose between:
- (A) Rescue southern countries (buying bonds, QE) → inflation lingers, EUR weakens
- (B) Let rates rise (defend currency) → southern countries refinancing crisis

Either way = volatility in OAT market.

### US Perspective:

**Treasury Yield Locked High signals:**

1. **Fed Balance Sheet Trap**
   - Fed can't cut rates without balance sheet losses
   - $5T in bonds purchased at 0.5% yields now in mark-to-market loss
   - Rates MUST stay 4%+ to stabilize the Fed
   - This is explicitly stated in their September 2026 decision

2. **Government Debt Service Exploding**
   - $40T debt × 4% = $1.6T annual interest
   - At 5%, goes to $2.0T+ 
   - 25% of federal budget eaten by interest payments
   - Either spending cuts, tax increases, or larger deficits

3. **Real Economy Deteriorating**
   - Diesel at $6.31/gallon = stagflation indicators
   - Consumer credit stretched
   - Recession signals building
   - Yet Fed can't cut = forced policy error scenario

**What Breaks:** Eventually (12-24 months):
- (A) Recession forces Fed hand; cuts rates despite balance sheet damage
- (B) Bond market refuses to fund deficits at any rate (Latin America scenario)
- (C) Explosive policy shift (austerity, defaults, inflation)

**Timing:** Less predictable than France, but severity likely worse.

## Tax Comparison: French OAT vs US Treasury in Belisimo

### **French OAT in Belisimo SAS**

**Structure:** Belisimo buys French OAT bonds directly

**Taxation:**
- OAT coupon interest: Taxed at Belisimo's IS rate (~25-27%)
- Capital gains (if sold): Also taxed at IS rate
- EUR-denominated = No currency gains/losses to track
- Straightforward accounting

**Example:** €100,000 invested at 4.945%
- Annual coupon: €4,945
- Belisimo tax (IS 27%): €1,335
- Net coupon to Belisimo: €3,610
- After-tax yield to Belisimo: 3.61%

**If held 10 years to maturity:**
- Total coupons: €49,450
- Tax on coupons: €13,351
- Principal repayment: €100,000
- Net proceeds: €136,099
- Belisimo holds this cash for further deployment (no distribution = no personal tax yet)

### **US Treasury in Belisimo SAS**

**Structure:** Belisimo buys US Treasury (unhedged or hedged via ETF)

**Taxation - Unhedged:**
- Treasury coupon interest: Taxed at IS rate (~27%)
- Capital gains: Taxed at IS rate
- USD/EUR gains: Also taxable at IS rate
- Accounting headache: Must track USD/EUR at transaction date, reporting date, sale date

**Example:** €100,000 invested when EUR/USD = 1.17 = $117,000
- Annual coupon: $3,276 (on 5.6% = $6,552, minus withholding if any)
- Belisimo tax (IS 27%): €884 (on EUR equivalent)
- Net coupon: $2,392 (net to Belisimo)
- After-tax yield: ~2.05% (but depends on EUR/USD movements)

**Plus:** If USD weakens to 1.30 over time:
- Initial: $117,000 = €90,000 (in EUR terms)
- If bond doesn't move, now: $117,000 = €90,000 (no gain from currency)
- If bond appreciates to $152,000: €116,923 (gain: €26,923)
- Tax on gain: €7,269
- Net: €109,654 (only +9.654% return, not +30%)

**Taxation - EUR-Hedged:**
- All currency risk removed
- Simpler: Just tax the bond return, not currency moves
- But hedging costs ~0.2-0.3% annually
- Net yield: 5.6% - 0.3% = 5.3% before IS
- After IS: 3.9% to Belisimo

### **Tax Winner: French OAT**

**Reasons:**
1. No currency accounting complexity
2. No currency gains to track and tax
3. Same IS rate but simpler compliance
4. Predictable returns

**But:** US Treasury unhedged gives 5.6% gross (3.7% after IS) if USD doesn't collapse

## Investment Vehicles for French Resident

### **Option 1: Direct French OAT Purchase via BoursoBank CTO**

**How to execute:**
- Log into BoursoBank bourse account (Compte-Titres Ordinaire)
- Search for "OAT" (Obligation Assimilable du Trésor)
- Look for 10-year maturity (closest to current yield)
- Buy directly on the secondary market

**Pros:**
- Direct ownership of French sovereign debt
- No fees beyond bid-ask spread
- No currency risk
- Full liquidity (sell anytime)

**Cons:**
- PFU 31.4% taxation (immediate, every year on coupons)
- Subject to French wealth tax if portfolio > €1.3M (though bonds have exemptions)
- No Belisimo compounding benefit

**Taxation:** 31.4% PFU
- Coupon: 4.945% × (1-0.314) = 3.39% after-tax
- If rates fall 1%, bond appreciation ~8%: 8% × (1-0.314) = 5.47% gain after tax

### **Option 2: French OAT ETF via BoursoBank CTO**

**Example:** iShares MSCI France (CAC 40 exposure) or similar

**Pros:**
- Diversified exposure (not just OATs)
- Lower fees
- Easier to buy/sell

**Cons:**
- Not pure government bonds
- Equity risk mixed in
- Less transparent about actual holdings

**Not recommended for this analysis** (you want pure OAT exposure)

### **Option 3: OAT via Belisimo SAS (Tax-Efficient)**

**How to execute:**
1. Belisimo opens corporate brokerage account (Saxo Bank, Interactive Brokers, or French broker)
2. Belisimo buys French OATs directly in secondary market
3. Holds in Belisimo account

**Taxation:**
- IS 27% on coupons, gains, and any currency moves
- Money stays in Belisimo (no distribution = no personal tax)
- Compounds inside corporate entity
- Only taxed if distributed to Simon as dividend

**Example:**
- Year 1: €4,945 coupon, €1,335 tax, €3,610 retained in Belisimo
- Year 2: €4,945 coupon, €1,335 tax, €3,610 retained
- After 10 years: ~€36,099 retained (after-tax compounding)
- Plus: Any capital appreciation stays inside Belisimo tax-free until distributed

**Pros:**
- Tax-deferred compounding (better than personal CTO)
- IS 27% < PFU 31.4% (slight savings)
- Can reinvest coupons without distribution
- Simplifies accounting (EUR-only)

**Cons:**
- Requires Belisimo structure (you have this)
- Corporate accounting overhead
- Can't access money without distribution (distribution = tax)

**RECOMMENDED** for OAT strategy.

### **Option 4: US Treasury via Belisimo SAS (Mixed Currency/Tax)**

**How to execute:**
1. Belisimo opens brokerage account capable of US Treasury access
2. Buys 30-year US Treasury directly (not ETF to minimize fees)
3. Holds unhedged (takes USD risk)

**Taxation:**
- IS 27% on interest received
- USD/EUR currency gains also taxed at IS 27%
- Capital gains on Treasury (if sold before maturity) taxed at IS 27%
- Accounting: Must track USD/EUR daily for compliance

**Example:**
- €100,000 @ 1.17 = $117,000
- Year 1: $6,552 coupon, Belisimo tax ~€1,768, net coupon €4,784
- After-tax yield in EUR terms: 4.78%
- If USD weakens 3% during year: Currency loss of ~€3,600 (taxed? yes at IS 27% when sold)

**Pros:**
- Higher gross yield (5.6% vs 4.945%)
- US debt fundamentals may be better (lower default risk despite $40T debt)
- Diversification benefit (different currency, different government)
- Fed locked rates high (yield sustainable)

**Cons:**
- Currency accounting complexity
- Currency risk is real (if EUR strengthens, kills returns)
- US Treasury rate duration risk (30 years = massive duration)
- Fed balance sheet deterioration could force policy shock

**NOT RECOMMENDED** unless you want explicit USD diversification.

### **Option 5: US Treasury EUR-Hedged via Belisimo**

**How to execute:**
1. Belisimo buys EUR-hedged US Treasury ETF via BoursoBank or other broker
2. Example: Lyxor 0-5Y US Treasury ETF (hedged to EUR) or similar

**Taxation:**
- IS 27% on total return (including hedge cost)
- Simplified: Just tax the net return, no currency gain tracking
- Hedge costs ~0.2% annually (built into ETF returns)

**Returns:**
- Gross: 5.6% - 0.2% fee = 5.4%
- Tax (IS 27%): 1.46%
- Net: 3.94% after-tax

**Pros:**
- Simplest US Treasury access
- No currency risk
- Liquid (ETF markets)
- Transparent fee structure

**Cons:**
- Hedging cost reduces yield
- ETF fees + hedging = less attractive than direct Treasury
- Less control over maturity/duration

**ALTERNATIVE** if complexity is concern, but direct Treasury better.

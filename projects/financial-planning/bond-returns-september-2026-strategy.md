# Bond Returns: Complete Framework & September 2026 US Treasury Opportunity

## Three Ways You Make Money from Bonds

1. **Coupon/Interest payments** — Regular cash income (e.g., 5% annually)
2. **Principal repayment** — Get your original investment back at maturity
3. **Capital appreciation** — Sell the bond for MORE than you paid if interest rates fall (crucial)

**Key concept:** Most people only think about #1 and #2. #3 is where bond trading becomes interesting and profitable.

---

## The Fundamental Bond Math

### **Yield to Maturity (YTM) vs Coupon**

**What ChatGPT says:**
> "10-year bonds yielding 4%" means: "I can lock in something around 4% annual return for ten years, subject to credit/default risk, reinvestment assumptions, tax and costs."

This is DIFFERENT from "4% coupon" because bonds trade at prices above or below their face value.

**Example:**
- Bond face value: €100,000
- Coupon: 4%
- Purchase price: €110,000 (premium)
- YTM (what you actually earn): <4% (because you paid more)

### **Future Value Calculation**

**Formula:** Future Value = Investment × (1 + yield)^years

**€100,000 invested for 10 years at various yields:**

| Yield | Value After 10 Years | Total Gain |
|-------|----------------------|------------|
| 1% | €110,462 | €10,462 |
| 2% | €121,899 | €21,899 |
| 3% | €134,392 | €34,392 |
| **4%** | **€148,024** | **€48,024** |
| 5% | €162,889 | €62,889 |
| 6% | €179,085 | €79,085 |
| 7% | €196,715 | €96,715 |

**CRITICAL CAVEAT:** This assumes reinvestment at the same rate. A real coupon-paying bond doesn't automatically compound like a savings account. You get cash coupons that must be reinvested at future market rates (which may be higher or lower).

## The Most Important Bond Relationship

### **Interest Rates ↑ → Bond Prices ↓**
### **Interest Rates ↓ → Bond Prices ↑**

**Concrete example:**

**You buy:** €100,000 10-year bond paying 4%

**One year later:** New equivalent bonds yield 6%

**Market reality:** Nobody wants your 4% bond when they can buy a new 6% bond

**Result:** Your bond's market price FALLS (maybe to €92,000)

**Reverse scenario:** If rates fall to 2%, your 4% bond becomes valuable, price rises

**Why this matters:** Long-duration bonds become VOLATILE when interest rates move, even if you plan to hold to maturity.

## The Duration Concept: Your Risk Lever

### **What is Duration?**

**Duration ≈ years of weighted cash flows** (simplified: think "years until you get your money back")

For a 30-year bond: duration might be 14-16 years
For a 2-year bond: duration might be 1.9 years

### **Duration Price Sensitivity**

**Rough formula:** Price change ≈ -Duration × Δ(interest rate)

**Example: Bond with duration 15**

| Interest Rate Change | Price Effect |
|----------------------|---------------|
| Rates fall 1% | +15% price |
| Rates fall 2% | +30% price |
| Rates rise 1% | -15% price |
| Rates rise 2% | -30% price |

**This is why a "boring" 30-year Treasury can actually be quite VOLATILE.**

A long-duration bond can produce 20-30% gains or losses based purely on interest rate moves, with zero credit risk.

## Two Different Bond Strategies

### **Strategy A: "I Want Predictable Returns"**

**Your thesis:** "I'll lock in 5% and collect steady income."

**What you do:**
- Buy a bond
- Hold it to maturity
- Collect coupons
- Ignore interim price movements

**Why:** If it's a high-quality government bond, you'll get your principal back regardless of market price fluctuations.

**When attractive:** When yields are 4-6% (decent income)

**When unattractive:** When yields are 1-2% (not enough income to justify risk)

### **Strategy B: "Interest Rates Will Fall"**

**Your thesis:** "30-year yields at 5.6% are unusually high. Eventually they'll drop to 3-4%."

**What you do:**
1. Buy at 5.6% yield
2. Collect coupons while waiting
3. When rates fall to 4%, your bond is worth much more
4. SELL and take capital gain
5. Redeploy proceeds elsewhere

**Payoff structure:** You get BOTH coupon income AND capital appreciation

**Example:** €100k at 5.6% → collect €5,600/year → rates fall to 4% → bond appreciates 25% → sell for €125k → €50k+ total gain over 2-5 years

**Risk:** If rates DON'T fall, or rise further, you suffer mark-to-market losses

## Reinvestment Risk: The Hidden Cost

### **The Problem**

When you buy a 5% bond, that 5% is NOT automatically reinvested at 5%.

Example:
- Buy bond today yielding 5%
- Bond pays €5,000 coupon in year 1
- Rates have fallen; bonds now yield 3%
- Your €5,000 gets reinvested at ~3%, not 5%
- Future value is less than the simple compounding formula suggested

### **The Solution: Zero-Coupon Bonds**

**What they are:**
- You buy at a discount (e.g., €61,400)
- They pay no coupons
- At maturity (10 years), you get €100,000
- Implied return: ~5%
- **No reinvestment risk** because there are no intermediate coupons to reinvest

**Advantage:** You truly lock in the return
**Disadvantage:** No income while you wait; illiquid; price very sensitive to rate changes

## When Are Bonds Particularly Attractive?

### **The Attractiveness Matrix**

| Situation | Bond Appeal | Why |
|-----------|-------------|-----|
| **High yields + likely falling rates** | 🟢 VERY ATTRACTIVE | Lock in high income + capture capital gains |
| **High yields + stable rates** | 🟢 ATTRACTIVE | Good income return |
| **Low yields + likely falling rates** | 🟡 POTENTIAL TRADE | Some capital gain possible, but starting yield weak |
| **Low yields + likely rising rates** | 🔴 UNATTRACTIVE | Poor income, capital loss likely |

**Dream bond environment:**
> Yields are high, inflation coming under control, rates likely to fall → you collect high income AND make capital gains

**Worst bond environment:**
> Yields extremely low AND inflation/rates rising → you're not paid much AND your bond loses value

## Individual Bonds vs ETFs vs Bond Ladders

### **Individual Bond (e.g., single €100k 30-year Treasury)**

**Advantages:**
- Know exactly when you get your money back (maturity date)
- Contractual, predictable cash flows
- Can hold literally to maturity
- No ongoing fees

**Disadvantages:**
- Illiquid (harder to sell quickly)
- Large minimum investment ($100k+)
- Requires picking specific maturity/issuer
- Concentrated risk

### **Bond ETF (e.g., €100k in long-duration Treasury ETF)**

**Advantages:**
- Diversified (owns 30-100+ different bonds)
- Highly liquid (can exit anytime)
- Low minimum investment
- Automatic reinvestment of coupons
- Professional management

**Disadvantages:**
- No "maturity" — fund continuously replaces maturing bonds
- Small annual fees (0.3-0.7%)
- Price fluctuates daily
- Return is combination of coupon + price changes + reinvestment

### **Bond Ladder (e.g., €100k spread across multiple maturities)**

**Instead of one €100k 10-year bond:**

€20k → 2-year
€20k → 4-year
€20k → 6-year
€20k → 8-year
€20k → 10-year

**Advantages:**
- Reduces risk of locking all money at wrong rate/time
- Creates regular maturity schedule
- When 2-year matures, reinvest at new rate
- Good for transitioning portfolio to income

**Disadvantages:**
- More complex to manage
- Requires active reinvestment decisions
- Higher transaction costs

## The Real Calculation: 30-Year US Treasury at 5.6%

### **Your Strategy Question**

> "If 30-year bonds are at 5.6% today, what happens if rates fall to 3-4% eventually? How much do I make?"

### **The Answer: Depends on When Rates Fall**

**Assumptions:**
- €100,000 invested at 5.6% in 30-year US Treasury
- Bond has duration of ~15 years (approximation)
- Duration formula: Price change ≈ -Duration × Δ rates

**Scenarios:**

| Rates Fall To | Rate Decline | Duration Impact | Price Change | Coupon Income (2 yrs) | Total Gain |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5.0% | -0.6% | -0.6% × 15 = -9% | +9% (~€9k) | €11,200 | €20,200 |

**Key insight:** A 30-year bond with 5.6% yield can deliver 40%+ total returns in 2-5 years IF rates fall materially.

### **But the Reverse Risk is Real**

**BUT:** Even if rates rise, you're still collecting ~5.6% coupon annually. If you hold 10 years, you collect €56,000 of income, which largely offsets interim mark-to-market losses.

## US Treasury Default Risk: How Real?

### **The Scenarios**

**Scenario 1: Technical/Political Default (Temporary)**
- Congress fails to resolve debt-limit issue in time
- Interest payment delayed by 1-2 weeks
- Subsequently paid in full
- Market shock, but no permanent loss
- Probability: ~5-10% over next 10 years

**Scenario 2: Economic Repudiation (Structural)**
- US announces inability to repay Treasury debt in full
- Permanent loss on principal/yields
- Global financial crisis
- Probability: <1% (extreme tail risk)

**Scenario 3: Inflation Erosion (Gradual)**
- No default; bonds paid in full
- But 30-year inflation averages 5%+ per year
- Your real return becomes negative
- Probability: ~20-30% over 30 years

**Assessment:** Default risk itself is low. Inflation risk and rate-risk are much more important.

## Your Bigger Risk: Currency (USD/EUR)

### **The Problem**

**Scenario:**
- Buy €100k Treasury when €1 = $1.17
- Cost: ~$117,000
- Bond appreciates to +30% in USD = $152,100
- But euro strengthens: €1 = $1.40
- Convert back: $152,100 ÷ 1.40 = €108,643
- Your €-denominated return: only +8.6% (not +30%!)

### **The Inverse Risk**

- Dollar strengthens to €1 = $0.95
- Same $152,100 = €160,105
- Your return becomes +60% (more than +30%!)

### **Solutions**

1. **Accept currency exposure** — simple, but creates extra volatility
2. **Buy EUR-hedged Treasury ETF** — removes USD exposure, but costs ~0.1-0.2% annually
3. **Use forward contracts** — hedge currency, but complex and expensive

**For your thesis:** EUR-hedged version isolates "long rates fall" bet from currency speculation

## The French Taxation Deep Dive

### **For Personal CTO**

**French PFU (Prélèvement Forfaitaire Unique):**
- 12.8% income tax + 18.6% social charges = 31.4% total
- Applies to bond interest (coupons) AND capital gains
- Automatic withholding + any settlement at tax time

**Example:**
- Invest €100k
- Sell for €136k after 2 years
- €36k capital gain × 31.4% = €11,304 tax
- Keep: €24,696 net gain
- Plus: ~€11,200 in coupon income × 31.4% = €3,515 tax
- Keep: €7,685 net coupon
- **Total net gain: €32,381**

**US side (Portfolio Interest Exemption):**
- If properly documented as foreign investor (W-8BEN form)
- US does NOT withhold on Treasury interest
- France taxes it all (see PFU above)

### **For Belisimo (Corporate)**

**French IS (Impôt sur les Sociétés):**
- Currently ~25-30% effective rate (depending on size/structure)
- Bond interest taxed as ordinary income of Belisimo
- Capital gains taxed as ordinary income (no capital gains rate difference)

**Key advantage:** Reinvested profits stay inside Belisimo without triggering personal taxation

**Example:**
- Belisimo invests €100k
- Year 1 coupon: €5,600
- Taxed at IS: €5,600 × 27% = €1,512
- Belisimo keeps: €4,088 (reinvested)
- If rates fall, bond appreciates €25k
- Belisimo sells, realizes €25k gain
- Taxed: €25k × 27% = €6,750
- Belisimo keeps: €18,250 (reinvested)
- **Money compounds inside company tax-efficiently**
- When eventually distributed to you personally, subject to additional taxation (but that's later)

**USD Currency Accounting:**
- Belisimo's books are in EUR
- When converting to USD to buy bond: accounting date has exchange rate
- When eventually selling: new exchange rate
- Gain/loss from FX enters Belisimo's accounts
- This affects taxable result

## The Expert's Staged Entry Approach

### **Why Not "All In" Today?**

Your thesis: "Rates will eventually fall from 5.6% to 3-4%."

But you don't know:
- WHEN this will happen
- Whether rates might rise to 6-7% first
- What happens to valuations in the interim

### **Instead: Staged Investment**

**Example allocation: €100k total budget**

| Rates | Action | Cumulative Invested |
|-------|--------|---------------------|
| 5.6% | Invest €25k | €25k |
| 6.0% | Invest €25k | €50k |
| 6.5% | Invest €25k | €75k |
| 7.0% | Invest €25k | €100k |

**Meanwhile:** Remaining capital sits in short-duration bonds earning interest

**Benefit:** You buy more at higher rates (better coupons), less at lower rates (wrong direction)

**This avoids:** Making entire return depend on perfectly calling the top

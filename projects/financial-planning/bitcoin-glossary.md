# Bitcoin Holdings & Leveraged Proxies — Glossary

## What the Article is About (Simple Summary)

**The Question:** Should you invest in **Strategy (MSTR)**, a company whose main business is owning 846,000 Bitcoin? The company is currently down 75% in price, but the article explores whether it's still a good long-term bet.

**The Simple Answer:** If you believe Bitcoin will recover over the next few years, Strategy might be attractive — but you should buy slowly (dollar-cost averaging) rather than all at once. The company is technically solvent but carries significant debt and relies on Bitcoin's recovery.

---

## Key Concepts Explained

### **Dollar-Cost Averaging (DCA)**
Investing the same amount of money at regular intervals (e.g., $1,000 per month) rather than investing all your money at once. **Why it matters in this article:** MSTR has fallen 75%, so buying a little bit every month hedges the risk that you pick the absolute worst entry point. It also lets you benefit if the price falls further.

### **Leveraged Proxy**
A financial instrument that multiplies your exposure to an underlying asset. In this case, **Strategy acts as a leveraged Bitcoin proxy** — when Bitcoin goes up, MSTR tends to go up more (because of how the company finances itself). Conversely, when Bitcoin falls, MSTR falls faster. **Risk:** The multiplied losses can be severe.

### **Bitcoin Per Share (BPS)**
The amount of actual Bitcoin that each share of Strategy stock represents. The company's goal is to grow this number over time through clever financial engineering. In Q2 2026, they grew BPS by 5% despite a bear market — a sign the strategy is working mechanically, even if the stock price is down.

### **BTC Yield**
The annual return in Bitcoin (not dollars) generated by Strategy's business. **4.5% BTC Yield year-to-date** means that if you owned 100 Bitcoin's worth of Strategy shares at the start of the year, you'd have gained 4.5% more Bitcoin by year-end just from the company's operations — independent of the stock price.

### **ATM (At-The-Market) Offering**
A stock issuance mechanism that automatically sells shares at whatever the current market price is, in dribs and drabs throughout the day. Strategy uses this to raise cash to buy more Bitcoin. **The mechanism:** Whenever MSTR issues new shares to buy Bitcoin, existing shareholders are diluted — BUT if those shares were issued above the company's Bitcoin holdings per share, existing holders actually gain exposure. It's accretive (adds value) or dilutive (loses value) depending on the price.

### **Modified NAV (Net Asset Value)**
The theoretical value of Strategy's per-share Bitcoin holdings plus cash, minus liabilities. If MSTR trades **above** Modified NAV, new shares can be issued at a premium and used to buy Bitcoin at a better rate. If MSTR falls **below** NAV, the ATM machine breaks — you can't issue shares profitably anymore.

### **mNAV Premium**
The difference between what the stock trades at and what its net asset value should be based on Bitcoin holdings. A wide premium is healthy (it means the market trusts the business). A compressed premium (or discount) is dangerous because it stops the accretive cycle.

### **Convertible Debt**
Bonds that the company issued ($6.7 billion) that can be converted into stock if the bondholder chooses. **Why it matters:** These are obligations — Strategy must pay interest regardless of Bitcoin's price. If the company's stock falls too far, it becomes expensive to refinance this debt, and solvency risk rises.

### **Unrealized Loss**
A paper loss on an asset you still own. Strategy booked an **$8.32 billion unrealized loss in Q2** because its Bitcoin holdings dropped in value. This is accounting pain but not immediate cash pain — the company still owns the Bitcoin.

### **Carrying Value vs. Cost Basis**
- **Cost Basis:** What Strategy paid for its Bitcoin (average ~$75,600 per BTC = $63.9 billion total)
- **Carrying Value:** What those Bitcoin are worth at current market prices (~$49.7 billion in Q2 2026)
- **Gap:** $14.2 billion underwater, meaning if the company had to sell today, it would realize massive losses.

### **Bear Market / Bull Market**
- **Bull market:** Prices are rising; investors are optimistic. "Bulls" believe in growth.
- **Bear market:** Prices are falling; investors are pessimistic. "Bears" expect further declines.
- **Why it matters:** MSTR is a "bull play" on Bitcoin — it only makes sense if you believe Bitcoin will recover from the current bear market.

### **Preferred Stock / Preferred Dividends**
A class of stock that pays a fixed dividend (like a bond) before common shareholders get paid. Strategy issued preferred shares to raise cash. In Q2 alone, it paid **$400.7 million in preferred dividends** — a real cash obligation even if the stock is down.

### **Bitcoin Drawdown**
A sustained percentage decline in Bitcoin's price. Bitcoin is down 46% over the past year and 26% year-to-date. A "drawdown" is just a fancy term for "losses accumulated over time."

### **Margin Call**
A demand from a lender that you post additional collateral because the value of your assets has fallen. If Strategy's lenders required a margin call, it could force the company to sell Bitcoin at the worst possible time. **The article says:** Prediction markets assign only a 3.6% probability of a margin call, so this risk is low right now.

### **MSCI Delisting**
Removal from major stock indices due to solvency concerns or regulatory issues. **The article mentions:** Prediction markets price in a 72.5% probability of MSCI delisting by year-end. This is a warning sign but not a near-term solvency threat.

### **Dilution**
When a company issues new shares, existing shareholders own a smaller piece of the pie. This is "dilutive" (bad) when new shares are issued below fair value. It's "accretive" (good) when new shares are issued above fair value and the proceeds are deployed wisely (e.g., buying Bitcoin when MSTR stock is overvalued relative to its holdings).

## The Bull Case (Why you'd buy)

1. **Mechanical compounding:** Every time MSTR issues stock above its net Bitcoin value and buys Bitcoin, existing holders gain BTC exposure per share.
2. **Analyst support:** 14 Buy ratings vs. 1 Hold; consensus target of $232.50 (138% upside from $97.33).
3. **Real cash generation:** The underlying software business grew revenue 54% YoY, generating $62.86 million in annual revenue.
4. **Leverage:** If Bitcoin recovers, MSTR will recover faster (multiplied upside).

## The Bear Case (Why you'd avoid)

1. **Massive paper losses:** $8.32–$14.46 billion unrealized losses per quarter; aggregate position is underwater.
2. **High fixed costs:** $6.7 billion convertible debt + $400.7 million quarterly preferred dividends must be paid regardless of Bitcoin's price.
3. **ATM breakage risk:** If MSTR stock falls below its net asset value, the accretive cycle stops.
4. **Stock collapse:** Down 75% in a year; delisting risk priced at 72.5% by prediction markets.
5. **Timing risk:** Betting the entire thesis on Bitcoin's multi-year recovery is a single-variable bet.

## What Should You Do? (The Article's Recommendation)

If you believe Bitcoin will recover over a multi-year horizon (2027+):
- **Use dollar-cost averaging:** Buy a small amount regularly rather than all at once.
- **This hedges timing risk:** You avoid the risk of buying at the absolute worst time.
- **Watch these metrics:**
  - MSTR trading near $100 par
  - The mNAV premium (is it widening or compressing?)
  - Quarterly BTC Yield progress

**The thesis fails if:**
- Bitcoin doesn't recover through 2027
- Convertible refinancing becomes impossible
- The ATM machine breaks (MSTR trades below NAV for an extended period)

# Tax-Free & Tax-Deferred Investment Vehicles in France (Sept 2026)

## The Bad News: Truly "Tax-Free" is Limited

France does not have a "capital gains tax exemption" like some countries (e.g., Singapore, UAE). However, there ARE strategies to **permanently defer** or **significantly reduce** taxation.

**Key insight:** Tax-free growth in France means either:
1. **Growth that is never taxed** (rare, specific vehicles)
2. **Growth that is indefinitely deferred** (reinvest, never distribute)
3. **Growth taxed at reduced rates after long hold periods** (8-30 years)

## Option 1: PEA (Plan d'Épargne en Actions) - BEST FOR EQUITIES

### What is it?

Tax-advantaged savings account for equities and equity-based funds. French residents can have ONE PEA.

### The Tax Advantage

**If held 5+ years: ZERO tax on capital gains and dividends**

Example:
- Invest €50,000 at age 45
- Account grows to €150,000 by age 50 (5-year mark)
- €100,000 gain = ZERO tax if you don't withdraw
- You can then reinvest gains inside PEA, compounding tax-free

### What Can You Hold?

**Allowed:**
- French company stocks
- EU company stocks
- Equity ETFs (UCITS only)
- Equity mutual funds
- Bonds are NOT allowed directly

**Not allowed:**
- Individual bonds (OATs, Treasuries)
- Bond ETFs
- Real estate
- Commodities directly

### Contribution Limits

| Structure | Annual Limit | Total Limit |
|-----------|---|---|
| Personal | €150,000/year | €225,000 total |
| EIRL (sole proprietor) | €300,000/year | €450,000 total |
| SAS/SARL | Various (company level) | Varies |

**For Simon:** If you converted Belisimo to EIRL, could contribute €300k/year to PEA

### How It Works for Your Situation

**Scenario A: Personal PEA**
- Open PEA with BoursoBank or any French bank
- Contribute €150,000 in Year 1
- Buy €50k France equities + €100k EU equities
- Contributions lock in for 5 years (withdrawal breaks tax-free status)
- At year 5: withdraw tax-free, or continue compounding tax-free indefinitely

**Tax example:**
- €150,000 invested
- Grows to €200,000 over 5 years (+33% return)
- Tax owed: €0
- Compare to personal CTO: tax would be €15,680 (31.4%)
- **Savings: €15,680**

### Restrictions on Your Bonds

**Critical:** PEA cannot hold OATs or Treasuries
- OATs = bonds, not allowed
- Treasury bonds = bonds, not allowed
- BUT: Bond index ETFs are not allowed either

**Workaround:** Use Belisimo + Assurance-vie for bonds (see Option 2)

### Pros & Cons

**Pros:**
- Truly tax-free growth after 5 years
- Unlimited gains (no ceiling)
- Can hold €225k total (personal) or €450k (if EIRL)
- Simple to open and manage

**Cons:**
- Cannot hold bonds (OAT, Treasury)
- 5-year lock-in (early withdrawal = tax + penalties)
- Only French/EU equities
- One PEA per person (can't have multiple)

### Action for Simon

**YES, open PEA immediately:**
- Contribute €100,000-€150,000
- Allocate 50/50 French + EU equities
- Hold 5+ years minimum
- This handles your equity allocation tax-free

## Option 2: Contrat d'Assurance-Vie (Life Insurance Wrapper) - BEST FOR BONDS

### What is it?

French life insurance contract that acts as a tax-efficient investment wrapper. Can hold bonds, equities, mutual funds, ETFs.

### The Tax Advantage

**Before 8 years:** Deferred taxation (no tax while invested, only on withdrawal)

**After 8 years:** Special reduced tax regime
- Capital gains: 7.5% tax (vs 31.4% in CTO)
- Interest: 24.7% tax (vs 31.4% in CTO)
- **Effective tax rate: 7.5-24.7% vs 31.4%**

**Illustration:**
- Invest €100,000 in OAT/Treasury via Assurance-vie
- Generates €5,600/year in coupons (5.6% yield)
- For first 8 years: reinvest all coupons, pay zero tax
- Year 8+: withdraw and pay 7.5% on gains, 24.7% on interest
- Compare to CTO: would pay 31.4% every year

**Compound effect over 10 years:**
- Assurance-vie: €100k → €182k (reinvested at 5.6%, no tax drag for 8 years, reduced tax years 9-10)
- CTO: €100k → €156k (same 5.6% but 31.4% annual tax on coupons)
- **Difference: €26,000 (17% more growth)**

### What Can You Hold?

**Allowed:**
- All bonds (OAT, Treasury, corporate, foreign)
- All equities (EU and global)
- All ETFs (equity, bond, mixed)
- Mutual funds
- Money market funds

**This is the big advantage over PEA:** You can hold bonds AND equities

### How It Works for Your Situation

**Scenario B: Belisimo + Assurance-vie (Recommended)**

1. Belisimo opens "contrat d'assurance-vie" as corporate client
2. Belisimo deposits €100,000 initial
3. Assurance-vie internally holds: 40% OAT + 40% US Treasury + 20% bond/equity mix
4. All coupons reinvested inside contract (no annual tax)
5. After 8 years: Belisimo receives withdrawal with reduced tax applied

**Tax treatment:**
- Years 1-8: Zero tax on annual coupons (reinvested)
- Year 8+: Withdrawal triggers reduced tax regime (7.5% on capital gains, 24.7% on gains above €4,600/year)
- Belisimo level: After reduced tax applied, money comes back into Belisimo and reinvested (deferred personal tax)

### Withdrawal Strategy (Key to Tax Efficiency)

**Option A: Annual Withdrawals (After Year 8)**
- Year 8: Withdraw €5,600 (first year's coupons)
- Tax due: ~€1,350 (24.7% on ~€5,600 gain)
- Net to Belisimo: €4,250 (reinvest for more growth)

**Option B: Partial Withdrawal (Maximum Advantage)**
- Let contract grow 8 years
- Year 8: Withdraw some gains, pay 7.5% tax (capital gains rate, better than 24.7%)
- Year 9+: Continue reinvesting inside contract
- Can do unlimited partial withdrawals with 7.5% rate after year 8

**Option C: Keep Reinvesting Indefinitely**
- Never withdraw (or minimal withdrawal for living expenses)
- Growth compounds inside Assurance-vie indefinitely
- Tax only on death (pass to beneficiaries at stepped-up basis)
- **Most tax-efficient if long-term (20+ year) strategy**

### Pros & Cons

**Pros:**
- Can hold ANY asset (bonds, equities, mixed)
- Tax-deferred for 8 years (powerful compounding)
- After 8 years: only 7.5% tax on capital gains (vs 31.4%)
- Can withdraw partially without liquidating whole position
- Beneficiary treatment (inheritance efficiency)
- Available to companies (Belisimo) not just individuals

**Cons:**
- 8-year holding period to access reduced tax (early withdrawal = 31.4% tax)
- Annual fees (~0.7-1.5% depending on provider)
- Slightly more complex than PEA
- Insurer credit risk (though French insurers well-regulated)

### Where to Open (French Insurers Accepting Corporate Clients)

- **Generali** (Generali Vie)
- **Allianz** (Allianz Assurance Vie)
- **AXA** (AXA Épargne Retraite Entreprise)
- **BNP Paribas** (BNP Paribas Assurance)
- **Crédit Suisse** / banking groups

### Action for Simon

**YES, open Assurance-vie in Belisimo:**
- Deposit €100,000-€200,000
- Allocate 40% OAT + 40% US Treasury + 20% equity ETF
- Hold 8+ years minimum
- After 8 years, withdraw selectively at 7.5% tax rate

## Option 3: Belisimo + Indefinite Reinvestment Strategy

### The Concept: "Never Withdraw"

Instead of optimizing for withdrawal tax, structure to never withdraw (or only distribute when you need cash for personal use).

### How It Works

**Inside Belisimo (SAS structure):**

1. Year 1: Deploy €100,000 (OAT + Treasury)
2. Generate ~€5,600/year in coupons
3. Belisimo pays IS 27% tax = €1,512
4. Reinvest net €4,088 inside Belisimo
5. Repeat: Every year, coupons taxed at IS but reinvested
6. Belisimo balance grows: €100k → €105k → €110k → compounding

**Key advantage:** 
- You NEVER distribute to Simon (personal)
- All growth stays inside Belisimo
- No personal tax until you eventually distribute
- IS rate 27% < PFU 31.4%, so already more efficient than personal account
- Can hold this indefinitely, pass to heirs inside company

**30-year compound effect:**
- €100k at 5% after-tax (27% IS on 5.6% = net 4.1%)
- 30-year value: ~€324,000
- Personal tax: Only when distributed (if ever)

### Comparison: Belisimo vs Assurance-vie

| Aspect | Belisimo (Reinvest) | Assurance-vie |
|--------|---|---|
| **Tax on coupons** | IS 27% annually | Deferred 8 yrs, then 24.7% |
| **Tax on gains** | IS 27% on sale | Deferred 8 yrs, then 7.5% |
| **Flexibility** | Withdraw anytime | Lock-in 8 years for reduced rate |
| **Growth after 30 years** | €324k (if reinvested) | €356k (tax-deferred 8 yrs, then 7.5%) |
| **Complexity** | Medium (accounting) | Medium (insurance contract) |

**Winner for long-term (30+ years):** Assurance-vie inside Belisimo (7.5% rate after 8 years beats 27% every year)

## Option 4: EIRL Conversion (Advanced)

### What is it?

Convert Belisimo from SAS to EIRL (Entreprise Individuelle à Responsabilité Limitée). Sole proprietor operating company with simplified structure.

### Advantage for Simon

**If converted to EIRL:**
- Can contribute €300,000/year to PEA (vs €150k personal limit)
- Simplified accounting
- Some flexibility on business vs investment allocation
- Still get company-level tax efficiency

### Drawbacks

- More complex to set up/unwind
- Less flexibility for multiple shareholders (Belisimo is yours alone, so less impact)
- Blurs line between business and investment

### Not Recommended Unless:

You want to maximize PEA contributions (€300k/year vs €150k personal).

## Option 5: PERP (Retirement Savings) - NOT RECOMMENDED FOR YOUR GOAL

### What is it?

Personal retirement savings plan with tax-deductible contributions but locked until age 60+.

### Tax Advantage

- Contributions are tax-deductible (reduce your income)

### Why Not for Simon?

- You want flexibility, not locked capital
- At your wealth level, income tax deduction less valuable
- Better options available (PEA, Assurance-vie)
- Only useful if you want to shelter very high income

## Risk Factors & Mitigation

### Risk 1: Early Withdrawal (PEA)

**Problem:** If you withdraw from PEA before 5 years, loses tax-free status

**Mitigation:**
- PEA is for 5+ year money ONLY
- If you need cash in year 2, use Belisimo or emergency reserve instead
- Mark PEA as "off-limits" until 5-year mark

### Risk 2: Assurance-vie Insurer Default

**Problem:** French insurer could fail (rare)

**Mitigation:**
- Choose large, well-capitalized insurers (Generali, AXA, Allianz)
- Policies protected by Fonds de Garantie (French protection fund)
- Maximum coverage: €70,000 per person per insurer
- Spread if going over €70k (e.g., €50k with Generali, €50k with AXA)

### Risk 3: Bond Market Collapse (Interest Rates Spike)

**Problem:** If rates spike from 5% to 7%, bond prices fall 15-20%

**Mitigation:**
- Long-term holding (5-8+ years) overcomes temporary losses
- Staged entry (deploy over 6 months) averages purchase price
- Keep cash reserve for additional buying if rates spike higher

### Risk 4: Tax Law Changes

**Problem:** France could change PEA/Assurance-vie rules (political risk)

**Mitigation:**
- Historical: These structures have existed 20+ years, unlikely to eliminate
- If changed retroactively, likely grandfathering of existing accounts
- Diversify across structures (don't put all in one)

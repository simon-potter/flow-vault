## Law #1: The Cash Waterfall System (Not 20/10 Rules)

**Myth:** "Save 20%, invest 10% in index funds" ← Sounds good, wrong approach

**Reality:** Wealthy people operate on a **cash waterfall** — four buckets filled in **sequence**, not evenly.

### The Four Buckets (Filled in Order):

**Bucket 1: Safety Net (3-6 months emergency savings)**
- High-yield savings account or money market fund with government bonds
- Must be liquid (get cash same day if needed)
- **Only real failure:** Bankruptcy from inability to cover disruptions
- Get this bucket full FIRST before anything else

**Bucket 2: Pay Off High-Interest Debt (>6%)**
- Exception: Only debt OK if you can return more than interest rate
- Example: Elon Musk + Apple have billions in debt → they earn 30%+ returns, so debt makes sense
- If you can't earn >6%, pay off ALL debt before moving to bucket 3

**Bucket 3: Invest in Your Own Earning Ability** ← WHERE WEALTH ACTUALLY BUILDS
- This is where wealth actually builds (NOT stock market index funds yet)
- Examples of what to buy:
  - **Leverage** (software, employees, contractors, systems) = Most powerful
  - **Skills & Knowledge** (ranked best to worst):
    1. Access (rooms with successful people)
    2. Mentorship/Coaching (from people who did what you want)
    3. Education (vetted courses/programs, not random influencers)
    4. Books (cheapest entry point)
  
- Why this bucket matters: Index funds give 5-10% returns. Your own skill development gives 50-500%+ returns
- Natural ceiling: Only so many mentors/books, only so many hours in day

**Bucket 4: Market Investments**
- Index funds, bonds, general investment portfolio
- Where bucket 3 overflow goes
- This is "passive" money (no longer actively transforming your earning ability)

**The Waterfall Order is CRITICAL:** Fill bucket 1 → spill to bucket 2 → then bucket 3 → finally bucket 4

## Law #2: All Wealth Comes from Inefficient Markets

### Efficient vs Inefficient Markets:

**Efficient Market** = Everything priced exactly right
- Example: S&P 500 (millions of analysts = hard to beat)
- You probably can't outsmart the market here

**Inefficient Market** = Information asymmetry + limited buyers/sellers
- Example: Water bottle at convenience store = $0.70 vs Mount Fuji summit = $15 (same product!)
- Same product, different market = 20x price difference
- The ONLY market where outsized returns exist

### How Wealth Gets Built:

**Strategy:** Take your skills/knowledge and move to less efficient markets

**Example 1 — Accountant pricing:**
- Regular accountant (efficient market) = $35/hour
- Specialist accountant for company exits (inefficient market) = $1,000/hour
- Same skills, inefficient market knowledge = 28x revenue

**Example 2 — Real estate:**
- Generic approach: Use realtor, buy "any" house (efficient market, poor returns)
- Specialist approach: Study specific zip code deeply, understand neighborhood dynamics nobody else knows (inefficient market, better deals, higher returns)

**The Principle:** Always ask: "Is this an efficient market where I can't get an edge, or inefficient where I can develop expertise?"

## Law #3: Build ONE Income Stream, Not Seven

**Myth:** "Average millionaire has 7 income streams"

**Truth:** Nobody became a millionaire FROM multiple streams. That's a YouTube lie.

### The Real Path to Wealth:

1. Pick ONE income stream
2. Become world-class expert in that stream (in an inefficient market)
3. Compound knowledge + expertise in that stream
4. Income compounds exponentially
5. **Only then** start investing in other streams

**Why:** You can't compound skill in multiple things simultaneously. Focus on ONE until you hit the ceiling of that market, then expand.

## Law #5: Think in Asymmetric Bets (Not Win/Loss)

**Two questions for every investment:**

1. **What's the maximum downside if this goes wrong?** (Can I reduce it?)
2. **What's the maximum upside if this works?**

**Goal:** Find bets where downside is small, upside is large

### Example — Richard Branson & Virgin Atlantic:

**Common approach:** Buy airplanes outright
- Downside: $50M+ loss if airline fails
- Upside: Potentially billions

**Branson's approach:** Lease one airplane for 1 year
- Downside: Capped at ~$2 million
- Upside: Potentially billions (same as above)

**Result:** Same upside, fraction of downside = better bet structure

## Law #6-8: Money Beliefs, Macro Thinking, & Historical Biographies

### Fix Hidden Money Beliefs (Law #7):

60-second exercise: Write "Money is ___" and complete non-stop for 60 seconds. Review each belief. Ask: "Is it true? Does it serve me? What else could I believe?"

Real truth: Money is a neutral tool. The tool isn't evil; how you use it determines value.

### Think Macro Before Micro (Law #8):

**Poor thinking:** "Should I buy THIS house or THAT house?" "THIS stock or THAT stock?"

**Wealthy thinking:** "Should I buy in THIS neighborhood/city/time in economy/sector?"

Macro-level decisions (market selection, timing, sector) determine destiny. Micro barely matters once macro is right.

### Learn Rich People Thinking (Law #6):

Read biographies of self-made billionaires from nothing:
1. **Total Recall** by Arnold Schwarzenegger
2. **Shoe Dog** by Phil Knight
3. **The Hard Thing About Hard Things** by Ben Horowitz

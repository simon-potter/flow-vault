## Science-Based Fat Loss & Mindful Eating

### Mindful Eating Foundations
- **Slow down.** Put fork down between bites, chew thoroughly (20-30 times per bite). Gives brain time to register fullness signals (~20 minutes).
- **Eliminate distractions.** Eat away from screens, work, or reading. Single-task meals so you actually taste and notice what you're eating.
- **Use smaller plates.** Psychological trigger — smaller plates feel fuller with same portion, reducing overfilling tendency.

### Breaking Snacking Habits
- **Identify triggers, not willpower.** Track when snacking occurs — is it boredom, stress, habit (time-based), or actual hunger? Address the trigger, not the symptom.
- **Out of sight, out of mind.** Remove tempting snacks from easy reach. Keep in cupboard, not on counter or desk.
- **Replace, don't restrict.** Swap high-calorie snacks with lower-calorie alternatives (nuts, fruit, veggies). Make better option more convenient.
- **Drink water first.** Thirst often masquerades as hunger. Have glass of water, wait 10 minutes before deciding if actually hungry.

### Practical Strategies
- **Scheduled eating.** Eat on consistent schedule. Skipping meals → intense hunger → overeating/snacking.
- **The 15-minute rule.** When cravings hit, wait 15 minutes. Often they pass once distracted with something else.
- **Prep snacks intentionally.** If snacking, portion first (don't eat from bag). Conscious choice feels different from mindless grazing.
- **Hunger hierarchy.** Before eating, ask: Am I physically hungry, or eating for comfort/boredom/stress? Real hunger grows over time; emotional eating is sudden and specific to one food.

### Environmental Design
- **Make healthy snacks visible.** Keep cut veggies, fruit, or nuts at eye level in fridge. Make default option the good option.
- **Add friction to bad snacks.** If keeping them, make inconvenient — locked in drawer, back of cupboard, or not in house at all.
- **Buddy system or accountability.** Track snacking for a week without judgment — just data. Visibility alone often breaks the habit.

### What Usually Works
Most people find success combining:
1. **Trigger awareness** — Why are you eating?
2. **Environmental design** — Make it harder to snack
3. **Replacement routines** — Do something else when urge hits (walk, text friend, stretch)

Willpower alone rarely wins. Start with one or two strategies, not all at once. Small changes compound.

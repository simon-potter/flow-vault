# Big Changes on Tiny Screens: How AI is Reshaping the Internet Economy

**Source:** PBS NewsHour  
**Video ID:** UXJd7mlUPTg  
**Duration:** ~26 min  
**Featuring:** Rand Fishkin, Caitlin Petre, Neil Vogel

## Executive Summary

This PBS segment examines how AI is fundamentally restructuring Internet economics, replacing the historical search-driven traffic model with AI-mediated access. While traditional publishers face dramatic traffic declines, well-branded publishers like People Inc. are thriving by:

1. **Licensing content to LLMs** as first-party training partners
2. **Blocking AI crawlers** to force paid access (Cloudflare blocking strategy)
3. **Diversifying distribution** across owned channels (TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, email)
4. **Increasing content output 50%** using AI-powered internal workflows

**Key insight:** The future may see AI companies like OpenAI/Anthropic building their own publishing departments and creator funds (similar to TikTok), fundamentally reshaping the creator-AI relationship.

---

## Key Points Relevant to Mediary

### 1. **Zero-Click Search is the New Default** (0:30 - 2:10)

- **Problem:** Google was sending 70% of searches to third-party sites in 2011 → now only 40-50%
- **Zero-click trend:** Search results answer the question directly without requiring a click
- **Impact:** Publishers relying on click-through traffic face catastrophic loss
- **Mediary relevance:** If Mediary positions content in search results without driving traffic to own domain, this is a feature, not a bug

### 2. **AI Companies Become the New Gateway** (2:15 - 3:00)

- Google Search is now "AI Search, through and through" (official Google statement)
- Content no longer needs to appear in Google results to reach audience
- AI chatbots like ChatGPT become the new question-answering interface
- **Mediary application:** Direct API or LLM integration becomes more valuable than web ranking

### 3. **The Death Spiral Risk** (10:00 - 13:30)

**Caitlin Petre's warning:**
- If publishers can't monetize search traffic, they stop creating
- Fewer new pieces → LLMs have less fresh training content
- Loss of local journalism = fewer sources for specialized knowledge
- Positive feedback loop of content scarcity

**Mediary concern:** If expert-generated content (legal, medical, financial advice) stops being created due to revenue loss, LLM outputs degrade over time.

### 4. **Licensing Content to LLMs is Real Revenue** (5:18 - 6:50)

**Neil Vogel's strategy (People Inc., 175M monthly reach):**
- Direct licensing deals with LLM companies (OpenAI, Anthropic) = **"material" revenue**
- Negotiated seat at the table before it became adversarial
- **Cash is cash** — doesn't matter if it's from ads or LLM licensing
- Example: 15 new avocado recipes, Kim Kardashian news — niche, fresh data is valuable

**Mediary edge case:** 
- Real-time, specialized content (market data, legal updates, medical findings) will be premium
- Commoditized content (generic advice) becomes cheaper as LLMs improve

### 5. **The Cloudflare Blocking Strategy** (9:40 - 10:00)

People Inc. partnered with **Cloudflare to block every AI crawler**, forcing paid access:
- Can't get content unless you license it
- Gives negotiating power with LLM companies
- Also protects from free extraction by competitors

**Mediary play:**
- If Mediary content is specialized/proprietary, blocking strategy + licensing = double monetization
- If Mediary content is broadly available, blocking is less effective

### 6. **Diversification Beyond Search** (6:15 - 6:45)

**People Inc.'s survival strategy:**
- Distributed 5 major brands across TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, email, product licensing
- 75% of audience from Google Search (2022) → now only 25%
- Aggregate audience **multiples of original size**
- 10 straight profitable quarters despite search collapse

**Mediary adaptation:**
- Multi-platform presence matters more than single search ranking
- Email audience is owned, not dependent on algorithm
- Direct product/subscription model de-risks content strategy

### 7. **AI as Internal Content Multiplier** (9:45+)

**Neil Vogel's operational insight:**
- Using AI to streamline 50% more content at roughly same cost
- **Humans write/create everything** (not AI-generated)
- AI handles editing, research, fact-checking, optimization
- Humans remain creative + quality gate

**Mediary opportunity:** AI-powered fact-checking, citation management, or expert network could be native feature of platform.

### 8. **The Future AI Publishing Department** (13:30 - 15:00)

**Rand Fishkin's prediction:**
- In 5-10 years, OpenAI/Anthropic will build publishing departments
- Creator fund model (pay creators based on traffic/training value, like TikTok)
- Fundamental shift: AI companies directly employ content creators
- "AI-shaped internet, built not only for but by AI tools"

**Mediary implications:**
- Current relationship (humans create, AI trains) may flip
- Dependency on single AI company's ToS becomes risk
- Creator compensation models will evolve from traditional publishing → direct AI partnerships

## Risk Factors (Mediary Context)

### Content Devaluation Risk

If Mediary content becomes part of public LLM training set:
- Loss of competitive moat (knowledge is commoditized)
- No direct compensation unless explicitly licensed
- Foreclosure from participating platforms

### Regulatory/Legal Uncertainty

- No consensus on fair use vs. commercial training
- Licensing frameworks still developing
- Small publishers have less negotiating power than People Inc.

### Audience Bifurcation

- High-quality, branded content → thrives (People Inc. model)
- Generic content → replaced by LLM summaries
- Mediary must be in the "branded expert" category

---

## Actionable Takeaways for Mediary

1. **Don't depend on search traffic** — expect 50% ongoing decline, plan diversification
2. **Identify licensable IP** — what expertise is fresh, proprietary, non-commoditized?
3. **Build email/owned channels** — reduce dependency on third-party platforms
4. **Clarify content rights** — decide if Mediary content should be blocked from crawlers or actively licensed
5. **Plan for multi-AI future** — don't assume single LLM partnership; prepare for landscape where multiple AI companies need training data
6. **Track creator compensation models** — TikTok creator fund → likely template for AI companies

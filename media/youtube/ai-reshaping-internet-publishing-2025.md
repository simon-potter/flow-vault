## Executive Summary

The segment examines how AI is restructuring internet economics, replacing the
search-driven traffic model with AI-mediated access. Traditional publishers face steep
traffic declines, but well-branded publishers such as People Inc. are thriving by:

1. **Licensing content to LLMs** as first-party training partners
2. **Blocking AI crawlers** to force paid access (Cloudflare partnership)
3. **Diversifying distribution** across owned channels (TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, email)
4. **Increasing content output ~50%** using AI-powered internal workflows

**Key insight:** AI companies may eventually build their own publishing departments and
creator funds (TikTok-style), reshaping the creator–AI relationship.

## Key Points Relevant to Mediary

### 1. Zero-click search is the new default

- Google sent north of 70% of searches to third-party sites in 2011; today that number is
  "in the mid-40s and falling" — Rand Fishkin, from clickstream data
- Fishkin dates the high-water mark for internet traffic from search/social/AI to **2022**
- Zero-click = a result that answers the question without any onward click
- **Mediary relevance:** if Mediary places content in results without needing domain
  traffic, this is a feature rather than a bug

### 2. AI companies become the new gateway

- Google's head of search: *"Google Search is AI Search, through and through"* — described
  as the biggest upgrade to the search box in 25 years
- Fishkin notes Google was disintermediating publishers long before AI (answering queries
  in-results, e.g. sports scores); AI simply enhanced that ability
- **Mediary application:** direct API/LLM integration becomes more valuable than web ranking

### 3. The death-spiral risk

- **Fishkin's scenario:** AI tools eventually can't find enough content to train on, so they
  build their own publishing departments. Within a decade or two, *"it would not surprise me
  if OpenAI or Anthropic looked a lot like TikTok"* — a creator fund paying by traffic and
  training value. The result is *"an internet built not only for, but by AI tools."*
- **Petre's warning:** falling traffic costs journalism jobs and shutters outlets —
  especially local ones — *"that diminishes the amount of content that these LLMs can train
  on too."* She frames it as a concern for AI companies and for democracy, not just
  journalists.
- **Mediary concern:** if expert content (legal, medical, financial) stops being produced,
  LLM output quality degrades over time

### 4. Licensing content to LLMs is real revenue

Neil Vogel, CEO of People Inc. (largest online and print publisher in the US, ~175M people
per month; brands include Better Homes and Gardens, People, Entertainment Weekly, Travel
and Leisure, Food & Wine):

- Licensing deals with LLM companies are **"real money… material for us"**
- They met Sam Altman at OpenAI in **autumn 2022**, before the first commercial LLM launch,
  and concluded *"the world is going to be different from here"*
- Their thesis: intangible brand assets **increase** in value in an artificial era
- *"AI needs three things: power, a model, and inputs. **We are the inputs.**"*
- Since the internet has already been crawled, value accrues to those still producing
  substantial new material

### 5. The Cloudflare blocking strategy

- In **July** they partnered with Cloudflare to block every AI crawler they could
- *"You cannot get access to our content unless you pay us for it"*
- Purpose is control and negotiating leverage — "a seat at the table"

### 6. Diversification beyond search

- Google search fell from ~75% of audience (2022) to **25%** today
- Redistributed brands across TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, owned email, product licensing
- **10 straight quarters** delivered on Wall Street; aggregate audience now multiples of
  where it started
- **Mediary adaptation:** owned email is algorithm-independent; multi-platform beats single
  search ranking

### 7. AI as internal content multiplier

- ~**50% more content for roughly the same cost**
- Crucially: *"humans make what you read and see"* — AI streamlines background processes,
  humans remain the creative and quality gate
- **Mediary opportunity:** AI-powered fact-checking, citation management, expert-network
  features

## Risk Factors (Mediary context)

**Content devaluation** — if Mediary content enters public training sets: loss of moat, no
compensation absent explicit licensing.

**Regulatory uncertainty** — no consensus on fair use vs commercial training; licensing
frameworks immature; small publishers hold far less leverage than People Inc.

**Audience bifurcation** — branded, high-quality content thrives; generic content is
replaced by LLM summaries. Mediary must sit in the "branded expert" category.

---

## Actionable Takeaways for Mediary

1. **Don't depend on search traffic** — plan for continued decline
2. **Identify licensable IP** — what is fresh, proprietary, non-commoditised?
3. **Build owned channels** — email above all
4. **Decide crawler policy** — block-and-license, or open?
5. **Plan for a multi-AI future** — avoid single-partner dependency
6. **Track creator compensation models** — the TikTok creator fund is the likely template

# Everything I Learned About Consulting in 15 Minutes

**Series:** #MondayMindBenders

**Presenter:** McKinsey/Deloitte Partner (12+ years consulting experience)

**Focus:** Deep career insights from 12+ years at Deloitte, running consulting businesses, and working in-house at Bank of America.

---

## Core Insights

### 1. **Two Power Maps in Every Consulting Firm**

**Problem:** People confuse firm power with client influence.

**The Two Maps:**
- **Org Chart Map:** Who makes internal decisions (positional power)
- **Client Influence Map:** Who clients actually trust (authority + influence)

**Why It Matters:**
- Firm map determines: internal promotions, leadership roles
- Client map determines: project staffing, re-engagement
- They often point in opposite directions

**Key Finding:** The best client-facing consultants (who clients request by name) often cycle in/out of firm leadership. They prioritize client value over internal visibility.

**Lesson:** Being known by the RIGHT people for the RIGHT reasons is the single biggest career accelerator in consulting.

---

### 2. **Reputation Is Three Distinct Things**

**The mistake:** Treating reputation as monolithic

**The reality:** Three separate dimensions

#### **Dimension 1: Technical Expertise**
- Knowledge, wisdom, problem-solving ability
- What clients want you back for
- Takes years to build
- Directly drives revenue

#### **Dimension 2: Work Ethic**
- Responsiveness, reliability, follow-through
- Whether you deliver on time
- **Can be built from day one** (even as an analyst)
- Example: Immediate response + estimated time + on-time delivery builds "dependability currency"

#### **Dimension 3: Relationship Skills**
- How you carry yourself with colleagues/clients
- How you handle hard work or uncomfortable messages
- Likability has always beaten being smart
- **Can be built from day one**

**Insight:** Junior consultants can have 2 of 3 immediately:
- Dimension 2 (work ethic) + Dimension 3 (relationships) from day one
- Dimension 1 (expertise) compounds over time
- Those with all three become "untouchable" and rare

---

### 3. **Zone of Genius = Deep Market Positioning**

**Problem:** Fear of narrowness leads to generalism

**The Reality:** Depth creates transferability

**Core Principle:**
> Clients hire based on evidence you've solved THAT problem before—not a similar problem, but that specific problem.

**Why Deep Specialists Win:**
- Banking client with attrition problem → hire consultant who solved attrition in banking
- Consultant cross-trained in manufacturing → client hesitates ("don't want to be where you learn our industry")
- Clients pay for existing mastery, not learning

**Paradox:** The people who became known for solving one specific problem were deployed MORE often across clients (not less).

**Reason:** They could go first/second/third order on any question in their zone. Full conversational freedom.

**Generalist Trap:** "Generally useful and specifically unremarkable" = competing on price, not authority.

**Right Shape:** Specialist enough that you're the authority on something + broad enough to understand business context.

---

### 4. **Superficial Knowledge vs. Deep Knowledge**

**The Trap:** Taking adjacent work from trusted clients

**What Happens:**
- You feel like an impostor
- Performance isn't great
- You have to control the conversation more
- Can't let it go where it needs to
- Shallow, superficial application

**What Excellence Looks Like:**
- Client can take conversation anywhere
- Knowledge just comes to you
- Deep application, first principles thinking
- No conversation control needed

**Especially True for:** Advisory work (vs implementation). You must know more than the client because they're paying you to advise.

---

### 5. **The Insecure Overachiever Model**

**Profile:** Relentlessly capable, always prepared, first to volunteer, last to leave, have an answer for everything

**Why Firms Love This:** Insecurity isn't a flaw—it's a feature they select for
- Self-managing
- Anxiety drives extraordinary output
- Cycle: perform → earn approval → reset insecurity (standard gets higher)

**The Dark Side:**
- Cycle never ends
- Question that dreads exposure: "Have you actually DONE this? Not advised—actually made decisions with your own budget/career on the line?"
- Performance excellence ≠ actual experience

**Solution:** Gap between advised and done is real and rational, but insecurity was pointing at wrong problem.

**Real Question:** Do you understand the terrain well enough to navigate a client through it? Are you honest about the edges of your knowledge?

**Best Consultants:** Don't convince clients they have all answers. They make clients feel safe enough to work through uncertainty together.

---

### 6. **Career Building Within a Matrix**

**Consulting Firm Structure:** Two dimensions with competing leadership/growth targets

- **Dimension 1:** Industry verticals (financial services, energy, consumer, government, tech) = WHERE you work
- **Dimension 2:** Service lines (strategy, human capital, transaction services, technology) = WHAT you do

**Critical Decision:** Are you building towards being an industry expert or a solutions expert?

**This decision shapes trajectory more than almost anything else.**

**Why It Matters:**
- Industry deep partners = years in one vertical
- Build relationships, learn regulatory/competitive environment
- Become genuine peers with clients
- Peer relationship generates revenue

---

### 7. **Specialization Creates Transferability**
- Spend years deployed across clients for one specific capability
- Learn that client specifics matter less than problem specifics
- "Knee is a knee" (same in China, South America, etc.)
- Geography doesn't matter if you understand the problem deeply

**Market Trap - Too Narrow:**
- Knee surgery only for 12-year-olds in Madrid = unsustainable
- Not enough clients

**Market Trap - Too Broad:**
- Generalist for hire
- Compete on price, not authority

**Right Formula:**
- Specialist enough you're the authority on something
- Broad enough to understand business context
- Can speak language of business
- Bring depth to one specific problem inside that business
- Confidence increases → changes how you show up → changes work quality → better results/referrals/clients → compounds

---

### 8. **Your Specific Depth Belongs to You**

**Realization:** What firm rents from you doesn't belong to the firm.

**What You Build:**
- Specific depth
- Problem-solving capability
- You carry it
- You can take it anywhere

**This is your most portable asset in consulting.**

---

## Key Takeaways for CTOs/Leaders

1. **Map power dynamics** in your organization (formal org chart vs actual influence)
2. **Build three dimensions of reputation** from day one (you only need work ethic + relationships initially)
3. **Deep specialization beats breadth** when positioned correctly
4. **Understand when to stay narrow** vs when to expand (zone of genius principle)
5. **Be honest about knowledge edges** (best leaders make teams feel safe with uncertainty)
6. **Choose your matrix path early** (industry expert vs solutions expert)
7. **Your expertise is your most portable asset**

# The Dip: A Little Book That Teaches You When to Quit (and When to Stick)

**Author:** Seth Godin  
**Published:** May 10, 2007  
**Length:** 96 pages  
**ISBN:** 9781591841661  
**Status:** NYT Bestseller (#5), 100K copies first month

---

## Core Thesis

**"Winners are really just the best quitters."**

Godin proves that winners quit fast, quit often, and quit without guilt—until they commit to beating the right challenge.

---

## Key Concepts

### The Dip

A **temporary setback** that gets better if you keep pushing.
- Hard phase that separates the committed from the casual
- The bigger the dip barrier, the bigger the reward for overcoming it
- If you can beat the dip to be the best, you earn profits, glory, and long-term security

### The Cul-de-Sac

A **total dead end** where more effort produces zero improvement.
- No amount of pushing will fix it
- Time to quit and move to the next opportunity
- Often disguises itself as a dip

### The Cliff

A **sudden drop-off** where conditions deteriorate rapidly (mentioned but less detailed).

---

## Decision Framework

**Core question:** Is this a Dip or a Cul-de-Sac?

If it's a **Dip** → Push through, persist, and beat it to become the best

If it's a **Cul-de-Sac** → Quit without guilt and find a real Dip worth fighting

---

## Why This Matters

Too many people quit **in the Dip**—the very moment they're on the edge of breakthrough.

They never reach the point of becoming so good at what they do that they can enjoy the rewards that come later.

The skill: **Knowing the difference before you waste years.**

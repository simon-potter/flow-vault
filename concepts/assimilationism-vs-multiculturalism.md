## Summary

In a FRANCE 24 interview, Alexandra Sabatu (National Rally MP for Pas-de-Calais) articulates the party's position on national identity and immigration: **France should pursue assimilationism, not multiculturalism.** The debate reflects a fundamental disagreement about integration: whether newcomers must adopt the host culture entirely (assimilationism) or can maintain distinct cultural identities within the broader society (multiculturalism).

## The Assimilationist Argument (National Rally Position)

**Core claim:**
> "The French Republic only recognizes the national community. We don't really care what's your religion, what's your origins. All you need to do is to live the French way. We're not like the UK with their policies about multiculturalism. We want assimilationism. That means that if you come to France, you want to live with us, it's okay. We will welcome you, but you have to live the French way. You don't have to import your culture, your way of living."

**Slogan:** "When in Rome, do like the Romans."

**Constitutional ambition:** Enshrine a "national community" principle to:
- Restore French control over immigration policy (framed as 30 years of EU override)
- Make cultural assimilation the law, not recommendation
- Reject what they frame as failed multiculturalism

**Key people quoted:**
- Alexandra Sabatu — articulates the policy
- Marine Le Pen — gave a speech on this vision
- Jordan Bardella — party president

## The Multiculturalism Critique (Implicit in the Video)

The interview frames multiculturalism (exemplified by UK policy) as:
- Enabling "parallel communities" without shared civic identity
- Tolerating cultural practices that conflict with French law/values
- Allowing religious/cultural identity to supersede national identity
- Leading to social fragmentation and loss of control

## Contested Example: Trappes

The interview addresses accusations that the National Rally uses dog-whistle rhetoric about the city of Trappes (a Paris suburb with significant Muslim immigrant population):

**Accusation:** Jordan Bardella called Trappes "the European capital of Jihad" and "a miniature Islamic Republic" (February 2026).

**Rebuttal (Sabatu):** The party distinguishes between:
- Condemning Islamism (political Islam, Muslim Brotherhood infiltration)
- NOT condemning Islam or Muslims as a faith
- Opposing cultural practices (e.g., forced veiling) that conflict with French law

**Interviewer's counterpoint:** There is a difference between criticizing extremism and characterizing an *entire town* of ordinary people as extremist. The distinction matters for fairness and accuracy.

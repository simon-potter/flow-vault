# Pension Age Reform Logic: The National Rally Claim vs. Reality

**Context:** Alexandra Sabatu (National Rally) argued that raising retirement age frees jobs for youth. This page evaluates that claim against evidence.

## The National Rally Argument (from video)

**Claim:**
> "If somebody started working in a factory at 19 years old, why would he need to work until 64, 66 like people who entered the job market at 23 or 25? We have a lot of trouble to find jobs for people in their 50s, 60s. The point of this pension policy is to make people work younger."

**Logic chain:**
1. People enter university at 18-25, delaying job market entry
2. Make retirement easier for early-starters (keep pensions generous)
3. This frees experienced older workers to retire
4. Younger people enter workforce earlier
5. Result: better youth employment

**Incoherence in the argument:**
- The policy is framed as both (a) making people work LONGER by raising retirement age, AND (b) helping youth by letting older workers retire early
- These are opposite policies

## Simon's Critique (unpacking the economics)

Your objections strike at three real problems:

### 1. The "Lump of Labor Fallacy" (but it's half-true)

**Your point:** Making old people work longer doesn't create new jobs; it just blocks youth from entering.

**This is the classic error:** Assuming a fixed number of jobs that must be divided.

**But the evidence is more nuanced:**

The IZA study ("Effect of Early Retirement Schemes on Youth Employment") analyzed 12 countries (Belgium, Canada, Denmark, France, Germany, Italy, Japan, Netherlands, Spain, Sweden, UK, US) and found:

- **No negative trade-off:** Countries that increased older-worker employment ALSO increased youth employment
- **Positive correlation:** The 6 countries with the greatest increases in older-worker employment saw the LARGEST youth employment increases
- **Germany case study:**
  - 1992 reform: raised incentive to stay longer → +15 percentage points for older workers
  - Result: youth unemployment DECLINED (not increased)
  - 2000s reforms: similar pattern

**Why?** Older workers are *complements* (not substitutes):
- They train younger workers
- They fill supervisor/mentoring roles
- Their continued work signals firm growth (not contraction)
- Continued employment = continued productivity = firm hires at other levels

### 2. But You're Right About the COST Problem

**The real manipulation Simon identified:**

When you raise retirement age, you don't save money in the medium term. You:
- Keep wages flowing longer (+cost)
- Keep healthcare costs longer (+cost)
- Keep pension accruals running (+cost)
- Create a larger elderly population that lives even longer (+cost)

**France 2026 context (from video):**
- Public debt: 3.46 trillion EUR (119% of GDP)
- Pension spending: 14.1% of GDP
- Macron nearly "doubled" debt (according to Sabatu)
- Raising retirement age was supposed to save 140 billion EUR by 2032

**But:** All French financial analysts (cited in video) said the savings plan is "partially works but contains vague and questionable elements."

**The manipulation:**
- Make people work longer to "save" on pensions
- But don't actually reduce pensions (Sabatu: "It's a choice, doesn't cut that much to keep the system")
- Result: people work longer AND pensions still cost a lot AND you have more old people

### 3. The AI Displacement Problem (your strongest point)

**Simon's insight:** Younger cohorts are getting displaced by AI anyway. Why force older workers to compete for shrinking jobs?

**Evidence from brain:**
- Young workers (22-25) in AI-exposed jobs face 19% employment gap vs. peers in non-exposed sectors
- Older workers in same AI-exposed jobs show NO gap (they're holding on)
- But this creates a blockade: entry-level roles vanishing, senior roles held

**The real problem:**
- Raising retirement age makes this worse, not better
- You create a two-front crisis:
  - Older workers can't retire (forced to stay, competing with AI)
  - Younger workers can't enter (fewer junior roles, AI automating them anyway)

## The Empirical Test: Does Raising Retirement Age Actually Help Youth?

**The OECD evidence says yes, but with caveats:**

**When it works:**
- Germany: pension reform → older workers stayed longer → youth employment ROSE
- System-wide: complementarity dominates substitution

**When it fails:**
- Spain, Hungary, Ireland, Norway: pension reforms coincided with older employment UP and youth employment DOWN
- Key difference: these countries had weak institutions, economic crisis timing, strong employment protection for older workers

**The missing variable:** Labor market dynamism
- If the economy is growing: raising retirement age + older workers staying = more total work, more mentoring, more growth → youth benefit
- If the economy is stagnating: raising retirement age = blockade, competition for scarce jobs, AI kills junior roles → youth lose

**France 2026 context:** Stagnating (119% debt-to-GDP, low growth predicted).

## The Real Problem With the National Rally's Pension Logic

1. **It's not addressing the actual problem:** Entry-level jobs are disappearing (AI + automation), not because old people are working too long.
2. **Cost manipulation:** Raises retirement age but doesn't actually cut pensions. So you pay for longer working lives PLUS you still pay for pensions.
3. **Ignores AI displacement:** Even if youth enter workforce younger, many will find those jobs are being automated anyway.
4. **Blocks a safer path:** If you want youth to work younger, fix education (shorter university, apprenticeships) — don't force 65-year-olds to work.

## What the Evidence Actually Suggests

**For youth employment to improve when raising retirement age:**
1. Economy must be growing (hiring at all levels)
2. Firms must value mentoring (older workers train younger) 
3. Technology must complement labor (not substitute)
4. Younger workers must have real alternatives (education reform, apprenticeships)

**France 2026 has NONE of these.** So raising retirement age is likely to backfire — creating the blockade Simon identified.

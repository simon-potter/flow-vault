# Paris Massacre of 1961 (October 17)

## Summary

On October 17, 1961, during France's colonial war in Algeria, Algerian immigrants in Paris organized a peaceful protest against a discriminatory curfew targeting Muslim Algerians. Paris police responded with violence: beating crowds, shooting, and throwing people from bridges into the Seine river. Over 11,000 were arrested; official death toll claimed 3, but historians believe dozens to over 100 died. The event was largely hidden from French public knowledge for decades.

## The Curfew (October 5, 1961)

**Trigger:** FLN (National Liberation Front) fighters killed 10+ police officers around Paris in weeks; police were angry and afraid.

**Policy:** Paris police chief Maurice Papon announced a curfew targeting specifically Muslim Algerians (who were legally French citizens):
- 8:30 PM to 5:30 AM
- "Officially strongly advised, but in practice enforced through arrests"
- Applied only to Algerians, not other Parisians

**Context:** 
- Algeria had been under French rule since 1830
- Algerian independence war ongoing
- Many Algerian men worked factory night shifts; lived in shanty towns
- Curfew treated entire ethnic group as suspects

## The October 17 Protest

**Organization:** FLN called a protest against the discriminatory curfew.

**Participants:** Tens of thousands of Algerians (men, women, children) dressed in formal clothes, walked into Paris.

**Weapons:** 
- Video claims: "told to come with no weapons and historians have found no sign they were armed"
- Described as "peaceful march"

## The Police Response

**Violence documented:**
- Officers charged crowds with clubs
- Beatings on streets and bridges
- Shootings (specific numbers not given)
- People thrown from bridges into the Seine river

**Arrests:** 11,000+ arrested and packed into sports arenas and holding centers; many beaten again.

**Death toll disputes:**
- Official claimed: 3 deaths
- Historians' estimate: at least dozens, many believe "well over 100"
- Archives closed for decades; bodies found in river for weeks after
- One written message found: "Here we drown Algerians"

## Timeline to Resolution

- **October 1961:** Massacre
- **Decades:** Event hidden from French public knowledge
- **1962 (9 months later):** Algeria won independence
- **2021:** French president acknowledged "crimes committed that night were inexcusable for the Republic"

## Key Person: Maurice Papon

- Police chief who announced the curfew (October 5, 1961)
- Ordered police response to the protest
- Never tried for the massacre
- Later convicted for helping send Jews to Nazi death camps during WWII

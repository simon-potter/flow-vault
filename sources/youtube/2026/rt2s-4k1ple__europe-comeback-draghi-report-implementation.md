## Overview

Progress report on Mario Draghi's 2024 strategic report to fix Europe's competitiveness crisis (383 recommendations across innovation, energy, security, governance, and financing). Two years later, implementation sits at only **15.7% completion**, with significant regional variation and backsliding in recent months.

---

## The Draghi Report: Three Core Pillars

### PILLAR 1: INNOVATION (3.7% Implementation) — CRITICAL FAILURE

**Draghi's Warning (2024):**
> "Europe lacks innovation. We're not creating tech giants like the US (Google, Nvidia, Meta, Amazon)."

**The Problem:**
- Massive gap in venture capital funding
  - **EU:** <0.2% of GDP invested in startups
  - **US:** >0.6% of GDP normally; peaked at **1.1% during pandemic**
  - **Implication:** US startups get 3-5x more capital per capita
- AI boom is entirely American (Nvidia, OpenAI, etc.)
- European companies cannot compete with US mega-cap tech
- **Consequence:** Gap is widening, not closing

**Why 3.7% Implementation?**
- Capital Markets Union (CMU) is supposed to fix this by:
  - Making it easier to move money across EU borders
  - Creating a single European investment market
  - Giving European startups access to €10 trillion in European savings currently trapped in bank accounts
- **Status:** Still in negotiation. National governments keep watering down reforms:
  - ESMA (EU financial regulator) was supposed to get SEC-like powers
  - Germany negotiated exceptions for its own stock exchange (last week)
  - Governments want to keep control of their own financial markets
- **Result:** Money stays in national silos instead of flowing to best opportunities

---

### PILLAR 2: ENERGY & DECARBONIZATION (14.6% Implementation) — MODEST PROGRESS

**Draghi's Warning:**
> "European energy is too expensive. This kills competitiveness."

**Progress:**
- Renewable energy share increased from ~15% (2024) to **~26% (2025)**
- European electricity prices have come down slightly since report
- **BUT:** Europeans still pay **2x what Americans pay** for electricity

**Why Still Problematic:**
- Renewables ≠ cheap electricity
- Europe's electrical grid is fragmented and under-invested
  - Grids not connected well across borders
  - Gas price spikes still drive up electricity prices
  - Building new energy infrastructure takes forever (regulatory delays)
- **EU Response:** Proposed "European Grids Package" (end of 2025), but still being negotiated
- **Timeline:** Even if approved, won't solve the cost problem for years

**The Opportunity Gap:**
American companies pay 2x LESS for electricity → Can do more energy-intensive computing (AI data centers, manufacturing) at lower cost → Competitive advantage compounds

---

### PILLAR 3: SECURITY & INDEPENDENCE (36.6% Implementation) — Best Performance

**Draghi's Warning:**
> "Europe needs strategic autonomy. Cannot depend on US/NATO for everything."

**Progress:**
- **Defense spending explosion:** EU countries will spend **€454 billion in 2026** → 2nd largest defense budget globally (behind US, ahead of China/Russia)
- **Real action:** This is happening

**But Major Caveat:**
- Draghi wanted **joint European defense procurement**, not just more spending
- **Current reality:** Still national champions
  - Germany ordering 35 American F-35 fighter jets for €10 billion (American, not European)
  - Only 11% of defense equipment procured jointly across EU
  - **Goal:** 40% by 2030
  - **Gap:** Still 29% away, zero signs of closing
- **Implication:** Europe is spending money but not building European defense industrial capacity

## The Financing & Governance Crisis

**Draghi's Estimate:** Europe needs **€750 billion extra per year** in investment to close the competitiveness gap.

### Sources of Funding Required:

1. **Private Investment** (majority) — But THIS requires CMU to work (see Pillar 1)
2. **Joint EU Borrowing** ("Eurobonds") — Would pool sovereign credit, spread risk
   - **Current status:** €50B defense loans + €90B Ukraine loan (temporary crisis measures)
   - **Permanent solution:** Nowhere in sight
   - **Problem:** Germany opposes joint borrowing (doesn't want to subsidize Southern Europe)

3. **EU Budget** — Commission proposed €2 trillion for 7 years
   - **Status:** Being negotiated DOWN
   - **Latest compromise:** Germany wants tighter spending; cuts innovation & defense; protects farming & regional subsidies (traditional spending)
   - **This is backwards:** Draghi says prioritize growth (innovation, defense); compromise protects redistribution (farming)

### Governance Dysfunction:

**Draghi wanted:** Qualified majority voting so individual countries can't block decisions
**Current status:** No movement. Commission is "looking at it." Will "take time."

**Draghi wanted:** Simplify EU laws/bureaucracy
**Current status:** Adopted "a couple of packages." Long road ahead.

## Implementation Trajectory: SLOWING DOWN

**Key Finding:** Europe didn't just implement slowly—it's **accelerating downward**.

```
Phase 1 (First year): 15-20% - "Easy recommendations" (low-hanging fruit)
Phase 2 (Year 1-2): Flat then declining
Phase 3 (Current): Actively slowing, backsliding
Overall: 15.7% after 2 years, trending toward stall
```

**Why?**
- Easy stuff got done (mostly symbolic)
- Hard stuff (actual structural reform) requires pain and political capital
- National governments protecting their own interests
- No political champion willing to force through unpopular reforms
- Draghi recently co-founded "Draghi Group" with business leaders (outside EU) to push harder—admission that EU process alone isn't working

---

## Critical Insight: The Geography of Reform Failure

**Germany's unique position:**
- Strongest economy in EU
- Controls ECB indirectly (political weight)
- Can negotiate exceptions for itself (e.g., stock exchange exemption)
- Opposes joint borrowing (doesn't want to help Southern Europe)
- Opposes aggressive innovation spending (prefers budget austerity)
- **Result:** Germany is effectively blocking the reforms that would make Europe more competitive

**Paradox:** Germany blocks reforms claiming fiscal prudence, but those same reforms would boost growth and tax revenue—ultimately helping Germany too.

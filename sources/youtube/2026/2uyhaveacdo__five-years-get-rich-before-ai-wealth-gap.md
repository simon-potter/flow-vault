## Overview

Mark Tilbury (personal finance creator, 40+ years business experience) argues that the next 5 years represent a **critical window** before AI-driven job displacement closes off wealth-building opportunities for ordinary people. He presents data on AI adoption, employment gaps, and wealth concentration to argue that asset owners will benefit while wage earners face structural headwinds.

**Key Claim:** The traditional path from poor → rich (selling your time/skills) is closing. Those without assets risk permanent economic immobility.

---

## The Core Argument

### Part 1: Historical Context (The Wall Between Rich & Poor)

**For most of human history:**
- Rich people owned assets (land, businesses)
- Poor people worked for them
- No mobility between classes (unless you married up)
- System was **stable but locked**

**The breakthrough (Industrial Revolution → Internet → Social Media):**
- Each major shift created temporary mobility
- You could sell your time/skills/knowledge to move up
- Didn't need to own a company—could work your way up
- Business owners + free markets knocked down the wealth wall
- Social media completely smashed it (creators could build followings, monetize directly)

**Tilbury's concern:** AI could reverse this progress and **rebuild the wall**.

### Part 2: Why AI Is Different

**Historical precedent (computers, internet, social media):**
- Changed HOW work gets done
- Didn't replace humans; just required new skills
- People adapted, new jobs emerged

**AI is different because:**
- Companies hire people because there's a problem that needs solving
- Receptionist needed → Someone answers phones
- Salesperson needed → Someone sells products
- Finance person needed → Someone manages books
- **Now:** What happens when AI solves the problem instead?

**The core mechanism:**
```
Problem remains
     ↓
Fewer humans paid to solve it
     ↓
Costs drop for business owner
     ↓
Business owner captures the margin
     ↓
Wage earner's leverage disappears
```

**Result:** Those who own the company benefit. Those who sell time get squeezed.

## The Data: It's Already Happening

### **Signal 1: Anthropic's Real-World Usage Study**

**Finding:** Claude AI is now being used for **at least 25% of tasks in roughly half of all jobs**

**Escalation:**
- One year earlier: Only ~33% of jobs used Claude for any task
- Now: ~50% of jobs use it for at least 25% of tasks
- **Implication:** Claude usage is spreading AND deepening
- **This doesn't include ChatGPT or other AI tools** — just Claude

---

### **Signal 2: Stanford Employment Data (Payroll Records)**

**Population:** Millions of actual workers tracked via payroll records

**Cohort breakdown:**
- **Young workers (22-25 yrs, AI-exposed jobs):** Employment ~19% BELOW where it would be if they kept pace with peers in non-AI-exposed jobs
- **Older workers (same AI-exposed jobs):** NO significant employment gap

**What this means:**
```
Older workers holding their jobs (for now)
     ↓
Fewer entry-level positions opening up
     ↓
Young people can't get the first rung on the ladder
     ↓
Locked out from wealth-building trajectory
```

**Crucial caveat:** Overall unemployment statistics look fine (no widespread job displacement YET). But that masks the crucial detail: **Entry-level jobs are disappearing while senior jobs persist**.

---

### **Signal 3: Business Profits Going to Workers Hits 75-Year Low**

**Historical data:** US business profit share going to workers via payroll
- **2026:** 52.8% (all-time low since 1947)
- **Trend:** Declining since before AI existed
- **AI effect:** This trend will accelerate (more output, fewer people needed, more profit → owner)

**Implication:** Even if you keep your job, your wage as a % of business output is shrinking.

## Why Nobody's Fixing This (Yet)

**Three reasons the crisis is invisible:**

1. **Entry-level gaps look like natural churn**
   - New grads not getting hired → Seems like regular recession/cycle
   - But it's structural (fewer junior positions, more AI doing that work)
   - Easy to miss if you only look at headline unemployment numbers

2. **Senior workers still employed**
   - Older, experienced people holding jobs → Looks like stability
   - But they're not being replaced when they retire
   - Younger cohort faces blockade

3. **Massive wealth effect masking inequality**
   - AI company IPOs creating thousands of millionaires (SpaceX: 4,400 new millionaires at IPO)
   - Money flowing to luxury real estate, services, consumer spending
   - Economy looks vibrant from top down
   - But structural opportunity gap is widening underneath

## Critical Interpretation for Simon

### What Tilbury Gets Right

1. **Entry-level automation is real** — Stanford data on 22-25 yr olds is concerning
2. **First-mover advantage is real** — Every tech shift has had winners and losers
3. **Asset ownership matters** — Wage earners getting squeezed vs. asset owners benefiting is structurally true
4. **AI tools are genuinely useful for solopreneurs** — The specialization argument is sound

### What Tilbury Oversells

1. **"5 years" is arbitrary**
   - Could be 3 years, could be 10
   - Nobody knows (he admits this)
   - Creates artificial urgency

2. **Event earnings claims need scrutiny**
   - $100M in tracked earnings ≠ $100M in profit
   - "Tracked earnings" includes gross revenue (before costs, refunds, etc.)
   - Selection bias: people who attend expensive events are already optimized
   - Not representative sample

3. **Historical shifts DIDN'T lock people out**
   - Internet automation made jobs obsolete
   - But new jobs emerged (e.g., "social media manager" didn't exist in 2005)
   - AI might follow same pattern (though risks are real)

4. **Assumes AI deployment will be uniform**
   - Some industries will automate faster than others
   - Some jobs will remain human-preferred (creative, counseling, high-touch)
   - Not all entry-level positions will disappear

### What's Actually Credible

**High confidence (based on data):**
- Entry-level tech/knowledge work is facing structural headwinds
- AI adoption is accelerating (Claude usage data is real)
- Business profit share to workers is at 75-year low
- First-mover advantage in AI tools is real (Anthropic guest is credible)
- Asset owners benefit more than wage earners in AI transition

**Medium confidence:**
- 5-year window is the right timeframe
- Ordinary wage earners should panic
- AI will create permanent underclass

## The Honest Read

**Tilbury's core warning is valid but overstated:**

✓ **True:** Entry-level jobs are being automated
✓ **True:** Asset owners benefit more than wage earners
✓ **True:** First-mover advantage is real
✗ **Oversold:** "5 years to get rich or be locked out forever"
✗ **Oversold:** Event earnings track record (selection bias)
✗ **Oversold:** Certainty about AI timelines

**The real takeaway:**
- The next 3-5 years are important for building assets
- But this has been true for centuries (compound interest works best over long horizons)
- AI accelerates the advantage of asset owners vs. wage earners
- But doesn't fundamentally change the math: start early, buy assets, diversify
- The urgency is real; the 5-year deadline is marketing
